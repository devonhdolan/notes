# Issue 177

Hello\! Last week was a bit more introspective than usual, which makes this preamble just the same. It really hit me that I’ve been repeating a line to myself, “This deal will close next week… next month… next year \- and then I’ll make the time to get to that personal pet project. That *story*.”  
That space is on the horizon, just out of reach. It’s visible, yet unattainable. In someone else’s hands, or not. Nothing happens as fast as you’d like in this industry. The cycle is vicious, a spiral, ad infinitum. The calvary is not coming. But complaining never did anybody any good \- therapy is pricey, but a bottle of rosé isn’t. Pity party :)  
I need to make the time to do what I love, and involve only the people who are essential to the process, who work on a similar timetable. Be very selective with your collaborators, the pot only shrinks and the levers of control get unwieldy. I wrote extensively about this in late Nov in [issue 165](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-165), and I’m disappointed in myself that I’ve yet to make a big dent in what I set out to do then. An empty promise that only one person can fix: moi. A personal game of decisions… dominoes.  
Next. I was recently reminded of the connection that Alison Herman made [in a piece she wrote](https://www.theringer.com/tv/2021/1/20/22239396/bling-empire-house-of-ho-crazy-rich-asians-reality-tv) for The Ringer which detailed how successful tv shows and features spawn reality shows. Desperate Housewives \-\> Real Housewives. The OC \-\> Laguna Beach. Crazy Rich Asians \-\> Bling Empire. It got me thinking about online/offline story dynamics, minting culture, and how you could take it a step further and lay a meta-commentary track on top of the drama found in reality shows.  
It could be something as simple as a weekly podcast that summarizes the events of each episode, or a livestream where the audience could be even more involved and share their opinions. Throw a turbo-charger on what’s trending, ride some coattails. Eventize the behind-the-scenes, take a 360° approach. You don’t need anybody’s permission. Have fun. It’s a form of multi-platform storytelling, I guess.  
Next week’s intro will be very thin. I’m going to Waiheke for a long weekend to drink wine and celebrate our one-year wedding anniversary. Yeah, we got hitched right as the world was set to end.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Finish what you begin. See you next week.
