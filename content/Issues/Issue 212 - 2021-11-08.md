# Issue 212

Hello\! Our relationship with art is dynamic, ever-changing. In some circles, a curious warp is taking shape. A behavioral shift. Creativity is being removed, and that notion is being accepted. Propriety is crestfallen. Machinations.  
A portrait of the non-existent artist as a young shill.  
A new craft. I won’t canvas all that I’m seeing, but some startups are modulating open-sourced datasets from the likes of OpenAI, Runway, Hugging Face and caking it onto the entirety of the creative process, productizing it, and calling the end result their own. Beyond that, they’ll offer it up as a subscribe-able service, beheading creative people and their professions along the way. Hello world, meet your maker.  
The symphonic computer — as composer, conductor, and orchestra alike.  
Short-circuit. I don’t think creative work should be fully automated (generated), though it can be augmented with technology, a tool. We coexist. Human in the loop, harmonize with machine. Visual effects did not wipe out special effects, digital and film carry on, as does streaming and theatrical. One might eclipse the other, but what is preferential is to be decided by creator and consumer. To each their own.  
The medium and format do not matter as long as the story necessitates it. Sculpt with word, image, or clay. Let it live. Provenance.  
Ownership of outcome is to be questioned, interpreted, outsourced. The engineer desperately desires the label, a brand, of being creative, but they’ve only painted their way into a pathetic corner. Every product has its own paw prints, but the person who made the brush did not Birth Venus (Botticelli). Frameworks aside. Art is intensely labored over, every detail, each brush stroke speaks. Full commitment, computation.  
Associative acts. Work has always been farmed out. Capital allocation all the way down. It’s gone on since the dawn of time. Gain recognition, then use your name as a storefront. Influence, don’t inspire. Damien Hirst would never, nor would James Patterson — I’m still surprised his BookShots idea didn’t really pan out, it went the way of Brightline. Amazon vacuum-sealed the publishing industry. A sad trombone plays in the distance.  
Economy. The content value chain is a spectrum between artist and audience. It swings, from one hand to the other, a synaptic connection. Connect your wallet directly to artist, that’s mass-market patronage… exit through the gift shop.  
The world is ripe with contradiction, it’s been that way since the beginning. Genesis rot, fin.  
Personal note: I’m cutting my (luscious) hair this week. It symbolizes something. A re-emergence perhaps. I’ve let it grow for almost 20 months, since the start of the pandemic. I’ll be donating it. If you know someone hair-impaired \- let me know, respond directly. Otherwise it’ll be going to Children with Hair Loss. I’ll post some before and afters on [my Insta](https://www.instagram.com/devonhdolan/) later this week.  
Speaking of Insta, thanks to you all who joined from there \- first time I publicly posted about my newsletter on IG last week. I’m not very good at marketing myself… I find it unsavory.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. L’embrasse de rigueur. See you next week.
