# Issue 186

Hello\! I’m scattered. I was pulled into a bunch of different things last week and this morning \- like being quartered by four horses running in opposite directions \- so not going to write a lengthy original preamble. Instead, copy pasting some lines (quotes\!) from emails I sent the past few days. It carries and applies across a couple projects. Work smart, not hard. Define what’s urgent and important, the Eisenhower matrix.  
We have to do what's in the best interest of the project, which also wholly aligns with our shared creative vision of the show. We must lead with the latter, the creative. That's where the ultimate strategic decisions are nested \- we have to remind ourselves that we turned down a submission to \[redacted\]. Would his name and credibility have gotten the show ordered, probably, would it have been the show we wanted to make and watch, probably not.  
I do firmly believe that past performance is an indicator of future success, as does most of the entertainment business, especially when a creative traffics in a specific lane \[genre/tone\] for a decade or more. What's the old adage \- you're only as good as your last credit? It is the lens by which many execs view the world.  
We need to really consider the X Factor, which would be when they totally "get it", they want to create it \-- they have an affinity for \[list of subjects\] \-- and even better if they've LIVED it. Authenticity is everything \- if our potential partners see themselves in the story, in the lookbook, that'll only bolster the appeal when we formally go out with it. It becomes way more personal.  
Quick aside, I’ve been (trying to get) in touch with a handful of foundations recently about an important and innovative project, and I’m now convinced that many are just charades. Shams. Publicity stunts. Those who manage them are exceptionally well-paid, they work for the wealthiest people on the planet, and more often than not I can’t discern what the hell they’ve done. Where are the results. All talk, no walk. A shame.  
Yes I know I’m generalizing, but it shouldn’t be too hard to point to projects that have been supported, especially when grant (free\!) money is being sprayed around. [MacKenzie Scott](https://mackenzie-scott.medium.com/) (Bezos) and [Jack Dorsey](https://startsmall.llc/) are super transparent with their philanthropy. I dig it. If you have friends that want to prove me otherwise, please feel free to introduce me. Sorry, this stuff gets me riled up, obviously.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. It’s a hustle. See you next week.
