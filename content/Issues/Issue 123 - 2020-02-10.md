# Issue 123

Hello\! First \- PARASITE\! So thrilled for this film to win. It is stunning and so well deserved. Bong is a treasure. This is history. I absolutely loved seeing people everywhere rejoice \- the happiness was infectious. Many of you were at the NEON party in celebration \- there was A.C.E. (K-pop boy band), soju (lots of soju), and peaches. Looked like one hell of a time. Wild to think that Tom Quinn started NEON a mere five years back out of the Cinetic offices after his Radius departure. [Here is an interview that just dropped](https://www.indiewire.com/2020/02/parasite-oscars-distribution-tom-quinn-interview-1202210320/) about their release strategy. Well done fam.  
Huge congrats to everyone involved.  
New York money people. Gotta love ‘em. Can’t live without ‘em. The rulers of the free world. The elite. These *sophisticated* financiers, who trade companies like cards, but don’t always know everything. I’m going to write a lil bit about Bluestar Alliance’s acquisition of Hurley from Nike ([press release on Friday](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/bluestar-alliance-moves-forward-with-plans-for-hurley-301000782.html)) and sponsorship models. I always lose subs when I write about surfing / sports (I don’t care), so if you’re not into it and think I have endemic Spicoli syndrome, then jump a few paras down where I write some quick notes on Hipgnosis Fund, Raven Capital, and others.  
I like to think that I’m still pretty entrenched in the surf industry. I’ve been catching up with a few buddies who are in it the last few weeks while I’ve been in Southern California. Many have started their own brands to pedal apparel, accessories, grooming products, and/or booze. Fun fact, when I was a teenager, in exchange for putting Reef stickers on my board, I’d get boxes of free gear and a small travel stipend—it’s all been downhill since. (The founder of Reef, who sold it to VF Corp in 2005 for \$187m, just got surfing into the Olympics.) I blogged about the action sports business fifteen years ago when I was a young buck. I wish I could find it. Think I nuked the thing out of embarrassment. Good riddance.  
Any who, so Bluestar Alliance dropped about 70% of the Hurley team after the acquisition closed. Surfing’s richest sponsorship deal came to a premature finish \- John John Florence’s eight years for \$30m. The clowns at Bluestar apparently wigged out that JJF was wearing a Billabong contest jersey, or bib, at the *Billabong* Pipeline Masters. They didn’t realize that other companies sponsor these tour events, each cost anywhere from \$250-500k. Must’ve missed that one in diligence. It’s like they’ve never seen a surf, skate, or snow contest before. Unreal. Many of the people in the industry believe that this is all a big bet on wave pools taking the sport global. Personally, I think action sports will remain pretty niche \- the industry fluctuates in booms and busts in \~15 year cycles. We’ve been in a decline the past decade, major consolidation.  
Finally, and this might be me being captain obvious, but I think many sponsored athletes are going to get cut across the board for every discipline. Big companies will handsomely sponsor a couple flagship athletes, for each int’l market and sport, and make a bunch of small bets on young-ins to see who pops. The flagship athletes will all have signature lines used to closely track their performance, ROI. Royalties. Top-line impact. Influence. Companies like H\&M and Uniqlo (Federer) will make their mark in action sports, Lululemon has a weird ambassador program that I don’t fully understand. Okay, enough of me being a sports junky. Thanks for sticking with me.  
Speaking of royalties \- [here is Warner Music Group’s S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1319161/000119312520026877/d805716ds1.htm) SEC filing. Last month I noticed FilmRise might’ve taken interest in Newmarket from Ambi (Bacardi $ and Raven Capital). I did some snooping, sleuthing, and figured I’d jot some thoughts down about media and entertainment investors. Raven made some noise last year as they acquired or took stakes in Open Road (from Tang), David Ian Prods (theater and live events \- Magic Mike UK), and well, Ambi. I don’t really know what they’re doing with any of them. Struggling, likely. In November, [I read about a fund called Hipgnosis](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/merck-mercuriadis-hipgnosis-songs-fund-is-already-worth-over-850m/) that’s buying musician’s catalogues. They seem to be doing well.  
There aren’t many talent participations buyout funds, which are generally built off the back of relatively “stable” income streams. A forecastable long-tail. Creatives have less control of their content (or none at all \- see T Swift and Carlyle Group), as publishing houses and studios own or license distribution. In connection with that, here are a few alt investment vehicles or funds that roll up participations: Content Partners (Wagner \+ Cuban, then Carlyle), Vine Alternative, Content Media / Kew, and Shamrock (Disney FO). Let me know if I’m missing any. There is Diamond Eagle Acquisition Corp, who just bought DraftKings, but they’re too complicated for my simple mind. The buzziest M\&E growth investors are Eldridge (Todd Boehly and a gaggle of ex Guggenheim investors), Chernin, and Raine. That’s a different category altogether.  
The pessimist in me thinks that certain financiers get into entertainment assets out of vanity. That’s okay, we need them. They want their name attached to a project, or a collective of creatives. Like Access Industries and WMG, but hey, they made a pretty penny off that one. There is no proven path in starting an entertainment finance company. The few models I’ve gleaned: pure development capital prod cos, production equity prod cos, slate funds, and the distributor that goes full-stack in success. Recent distribs that have evolved into full-fledged studios include A24, and NEON. Others that are pursuing that track: Bleecker Street, FilmRise. Some failed awhile back, like Millennium and Alchemy. A lot of success was found 15 years ago if you managed to wrangle a slate deal with a studio while the economy collapsed. If you did this you probably made out like gangbusters, see Legendary, but I heard that Wanda wrote down one, and then there was the collapse of Relativity (now owned by Ultra V). Anton Corp is doing something similar now in Europe with Studio Canal. A couple of you know way more about this space than I do, please feel free to correct anything that I wrote and I’ll amend and shout in next issue. It’s all so unpredictable.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
