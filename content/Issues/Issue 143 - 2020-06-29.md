# Issue 143

Hello\! Broken systems. They’re around everywhere. I can’t stop thinking about them. The one I find most important, education. To get to the point of my soliloquy if you want to go dive headfirst into the links, I think we need to implement more apprenticeship and mentorship programs in middle school and high school. Big Brothers Big Sisters. But focused on professional prospects. C’est tout.  
It’s wild that those who are “gifted” at a young age are put in advanced placement programs… the promised land of math, music, and sports. It’s relatively simple to understand what it means to make it in the last two \- the big leagues \- but most other fields are hidden in shadow. We need to make it simpler to unlock opportunity, to educate the youth \- the next generation : the future \- on the real-world applications of their learnings. Putting principles into practice.  
There are a few new developments like accelerators, academies, and fellowships for people in their 20s and beyond, but we need to start earlier. And I think we should do it for the good of society, no strings attached. Most of these paid bootcamps expect their clients’ future earnings to line their pockets as well. Now imagine schools expecting the same. Oh wait, that’s part of the wonderful student loan industrial complex. Capitalism sure is tasty, isn’t it. Moving on.  
Parents and children alike chase the pedigree’d rubber stamp of high-brow institutions, as they bear a more “proven” script to success. They’re brands that you sought out when you nailed a standardized test and wrote a cute essay at seventeen. Some candidates might’ve peaked early, but hey, they certainly got a leg up on the competition. You can lead a horse to water…  
If only reputation was centered in action, not perception.  
So many of the well-regarded, fanciful careers are held by the advantaged, and deeply protected by them. I had no idea what what an investment banker or management consultant did until I was about 21\. That’s what Bud Fox did, right? Same goes for all careers in high-finance. Too little too late. Oh well. That’s on me.  
Hell, it seems that at most jobs they expect to re-build you for first 1-3 months of your stint, like the Bionic Man. These are the in-house bootcamps. The hazing, initiation period. Make it or break it. Sink or swim. Both parties win. Good game. Dual returns.  
Let’s create systems that compound positively. Double bottom line.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
