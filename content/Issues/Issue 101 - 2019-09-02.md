# Issue 101

Hello\! Today marks the last day of summer. It’s blue skies and sunshine here in Laguna Beach. I’m going to miss this place. This is my last week home in LA/OC before moving back to NYC. More on that next week. I’m about to head to the beach to go surf, swim, and play. Speaking of, this week three tech-focused newsletters I follow linked to articles about surfing. Two highlighted the contest in Tahiti ([Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/photo/2019/08/surfing-teahupoo-photos-from-above-and-below/596905/)), and another of surfing in the age of the omnipresent camera ([NewYorker](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/culture-desk/surfing-in-the-age-of-the-surfing-selfie)). I thought to myself: perhaps surf culture is actually undergoing some sort of renaissance. It was only a few months ago I waxed poetic about how the action sports industry hugely influenced my adolescence, and that it felt like it was on the steep decline amongst today’s youth. I only hoped that I was wrong, and I was happily met with some counter-proof. One fun thing to note is that literally every surfer is an amateur meteorologist, no joke. They’re always forecasting surf and swell conditions, talking about the contours of the ocean bottom and it’s makeup (sand, reef, rock), and taking into account the depth of the ocean floor and it’s proximity to the break-line. Marine meteorology, or oceanography, is the study of weather (wind, offshore storms), tide, and swell directions. I grew up checking a site called [Surfline](https://www.surfline.com/) on the daily, and still do to an extent. It’s now a bit clunky, but 20 years ago it was the pinnacle of cutting-edge. The company operates one of the largest networks of internet-enabled live video cameras, publishes decent editorial content, and probably employs the largest group of oceanographers in the world. The company collected Webby Awards year after year in the late 90s and early 00s, and I can certainly say that it helped shape my idea of the possibilities of the internet. That, and well, Napster. I was talking to a friend about all this stuff last week and his eyes lit up, so I figured it might be interesting enough to share here. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
