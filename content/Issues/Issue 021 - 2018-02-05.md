# Issue 21

Hello\! Super Bowl activities interfered with my Sunday writing time for temee.co, so let’s talk about that. To me, the Super Bowl is this once a year phenomena where people actually care about commercials. Incredible. How systemically broken is an industry where people want to seek out your product just once a year? One that distracts from, and interrupts, the main attraction? Yes, I generally have a (very) pessimistic outlook on the traditional advertising business. It’s antiquated, problematic, and in decay. Like, the DVR was built to avoid commercials, and obviously to allow people to watch film/TV around its programmed schedule. My main contention with advertising is that I think ad agencies usually miss the point, especially for CPG companies. Companies need to focus on the brand’s overall story, mission, and purpose, not on positioning and selling a product. The latter feeds on people’s desires and consumerist instincts, which creates negative societal impact. Worse yet, are the silly, gimmicky spots like Puppy Monkey Baby (ew). Seriously, what does that contribute to your brand messaging?\! The solution for brands, and it’s one that we’re working on at Cinetic, is to re-imagine themselves as producers rather than marketers, and to make structured investments in long-form content (film/TV). If the film/TV show is popular, it will lead to cultural conversation around a brand’s ethos, which is invaluable. But wait, there’s more\! If the the film/TV show is acquired, the company might actually recoup its marketing “spend,” which is probably foreign for most marketing departments. I firmly believe there will be tectonic shifts in this space in the near future, especially now that many content providers and distributors are pure-play marketplaces and storefronts (e.g. Amazon). Disagree? Agree? I’d love to hear what you think\!
