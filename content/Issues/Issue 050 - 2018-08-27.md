# Issue 50

Hello\! Keeping this super light-touch and stream of consciousness because it’s the last official week of summer and most people are checked out. I saw 2001: A Space Odyssey in IMAX 70mm on Friday night. It was cosmically biblical. Kubrick is my favorite director of all time. Christopher Nolan oversaw the making of this “un-restored” version from an original print. The run is going for another couple of days and I’d definitely recommend it. It’s an experience. Influential. Beautiful. Spectacle. Expensive. The audience was perfect for it too. Adult. Appreciative. Non-interruptive. Engaged as one. Nolan is one of the few filmmakers where I’ll make an effort to watch his movies in IMAX. Last year I caught Blade Runner 2049 and Dunkirk in IMAX. Both were breathtaking. Blade Runner was a bday present from my partner in crime, Anmari. We walked around Times Square afterward and it felt like we were living in the movie. That’s a memory I’ll savor forever… and it took place in Times Square — who’da thunk it. I watch about 2–3 movies a week in theater. I don’t use Moviepass because I don’t want to give them a dime of my money, but really I did them a favor for not subscribing. I often see movies down the street from me at a place called Syndicated BK. It’s one of my fave cinemas in NYC. They care. It’s “second-run” in a hipster neighborhood, so distributors like word-of-mouth from it. Thanks to the collapsing of windows, “indie” films screen there roughly a month after their opening. They also show the classics regularly, and poll the audience on what to schedule for the following month. Convenient. Cheap. Perfect. Alas, I don’t think every movie is theatrical. As I see it, it’s not a tent-pole vs streaming argument, it’s about a film’s tone. Its audiovisual nature. Aesthetic. Again, spectacle. That’s all for now. See you next week\!
