# Issue 245

Hello\! People need to put their tastes, their inputs, on display. Recommendations are a representation of self. Let’s start easy: flex your friends – they win, you win. The old expression says you’re the average of the 5 people you spend the most time with. Guess that just makes me a reflection of my one and only \<3  
What do you love? Flaunt it. Care, more. Do better. I’m trying.  
Gone are the days of proudly highlighting your friend’s sites on your blog. Pour one out for the Myspace Top 8\. Substack’s new writer referral program is neat and already driving network effects. If you want to be product manager-y, call it intra-network cross-pollination.  
It’s the game of attribution & affiliation… of association. Deep links. Deep cuts.  
I’m on the constant lookout for how art and information is shared, especially online. I’ve made it my side hobby the last five years… and I hardly see any of it. Especially by representatives, it’s a joke. Hell, many firms don’t showcase their clients on their site, but you’ll be sure to find an elaborate About page detailing senior leadership’s whereabouts. The random agent or studio exec would prefer to tag Telluride Film Festival — just to let others know they’re there — than share the films they found pleasurable. I am guilty of this. It’s the social media Me Show, perfect for peacocking and clout chasing. Everything is *your* platform, *your* pedestal.  
Status; Signals. People would rather tag The Strand than read a book.  
The quest to discover the artist’s artist, or in today’s parlance, the influencer’s influencer.  
Who is actually moving the social consciousness? Setting the stage, the strategy?  
The industry’s inner sanctum is full of its curators, creators, & rainmakers. The leaders of the ‘free’ world are all three. It’s built around reputation and relationships. Ultimately, taste and ideas (and money) are your edge, your differentiator. Film festivals market in just this.    
Film & TV people have made a career, a tiny casino, out of it. The walls to this industry have been coming down for a few years now, but inflated egos and obliviousness runs rampant. People are unaware of what’s around the corner. Time is your enemy, and it’s business affairs’ ally. You don’t sway talent by first convincing their agent or manager of your project, you do it with their closest friends, business partners, and employees.  
Take the side door. Go straight to the source. We’re all adults here.  
You’d be surprised at just how many creatives aren’t satisfied with their reps. I’ve heard complaints from the biggest actors in the world, the last bastion of bankable talent, that they don’t feel serviced. Gross negligence. Same goes for portfolio cos and their ‘value add’ VCs. Do the work.  
Take responsibility. Put your reputation on the line with every submission email you send. It just takes one email to change your life.  
The scene is dead, long live the scene. Add it to the ever-growing queue. We’ll talk “impressions” next week… and forum in-jokes.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Deluge of disposability. See ya next week. 5 left.
