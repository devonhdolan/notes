# Issue 179

Hello\! So where was I. [Last week I mentioned](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-178-3-15-21-1899575aa83c?source=friends_link&sk=7da89e35b778f4b55b0c82c89409c3bb) today’s rant, and next week’s, will be part of a loosely threaded series around the convergence of creative “accelerators,” online media (DTC), and emerging technologies. These shared themes have been top of mind since I got my start in the industry \- they’ve informed my perspective on where I think it’s headed more than anything else. The game is being re-written. I’ll also draft off [issue 175](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-175-2-22-21-a13495baf544?source=friends_link&sk=0e22c53c4bd5278c9922c8da5bff415b) that was published exactly a month ago that touched on NFTs \- technology as a tool, not the answer. Okay, scattered thoughts and dovetails.  
The iPhone and Internet birthed a whole new creator class (one that’s subjugated to the algo), and it seems the end goal for many digital-first creators is to end up on a traditional format \- though I’d wager that trend line will curve downward as legacy loses luster. It languishes. Or perhaps it’ll split into two. Traditional will morph into a hybrid offering where “the meat” of a show is also available online… on social media, on the show’s account and also the individual’s. A fissure will grow. This dynamic currently favors comedians and talk show hosts, which is much less capital intensive and has a quicker turnaround rate than say making a feature or scripted series. Though in our streaming era, what’s less disposable.  
The audience is more in control today \- they want content anywhere, anytime \- and their attention span is at most 3 seconds. A near unsolvable problem. They vote with their attention, and wallets. The widely-followed personality-driven creators will demand to host distribution in lockstep with their traditional partner, if they have one. Just look at The Daily Show’s approach, and literally any other hosted show. That model is going to become more commonplace. Other examples that harken back to the prior paragraph include Lilly Singh (YouTube to NBC) and Sarah Cooper (TikTok to Netflix and CBS). Maybe other digital creators think they’ll get lucky and think a film/tv deal will be a happenstance byproduct of their creative output. It’s not guaranteed, so why waste time chasing it \- I should repeat that one to myself. To that end, I don’t know how this’ll work for longer form *stories*, but I do think there’ll be a wave of experimentation here, beyond just the trailer sharing on social we’re seeing now.  
When I hear the phrase *creator economy,* generally the types of ‘Internet creatives’ that spring to mind are vloggers, game streamers, newsletter writers (shit), interview podcasters, bloggers, product reviewers, memers, comedy sketches, hotties with bodies, etc. Derogatory. Usually they’re parties of one who control creation and self-distribute on some social channel (or all of them) with a tip jar on Patreon. These are near borderline inter-changeable with the influencers of yesteryear, the new self-sponsored merch peddlers. The term influencer had a negative connotation, so creator / passion economy was dubbed to add more regal flair to the space. Their preferred formats are often considered low hanging fruit by Hollywood development execs \- yet short stories on Reddit and narrative podcasts are proclaimed as the new creative breeding ground. Anyone can do it, but most don’t. Paralyzed by particularity. PS \- most Hollywood execs haven’t created jack.  
It’s funny, that creator economy phrase seems to be mostly perpetuated by tech reporters, investors, and and execs who willfully forget that’s the purpose of the entire entertainment industry. It is *the* creator economy, and it is adopting digital more each and everyday. There are plenty of benefits of being an online media creator, the biggest is that you own the relationship with your audience, that’s the direct-to-consumer (DTC) aspect. But to have staying power, you’ve gotta post consistently and those posts needs to be half-decent, or rather, what the audience expects of you. Cater (pander?) for engagement. It’s an attractive occupation, that begins as a vocation. Content creator careerism.  
Creator Economy. Entertainment Industry. You know, there’s a shared goal — it’s to get great projects made and creators paid. That’s it. The interests of all parties should be aligned at the offset, both creative and financial. I’m sure that’s what every digital-first creator believes too. Hell, same with some of the smarmier, self-serving intermediaries \- they’ll just be first in line to the buffet table for their commish. I think they’ll eventually fall by the wayside if they’re not additive. That’s the social contract that’s principally agreed to by the system. It can be written by lawyers, or democratically agreed to on-chain. That last one is literal economy creation, by creatives. I’ll touch more on that next week \- very excited by the prospects of it.  
The path to becoming a digital-first creator is the one with the least resistance (I’ve stupidly chosen the opposite with all my projects). Half the battle is doing it, the other is making it \- which to me, is generating enough income as an individual to cover your overhead, the essentials. The hard part is building an audience (solve that dilemma more broadly, and you’ll do well) and getting noticed by the tastemakers, who are curators in and of themselves. The tools are right in front of you, assuming you have Internet and an iPhone. What are you waiting for. Prove yourself.  
Well, I had something totally different drafted and planned for today (and a bit more innovative), but I went off-rails and punched this diatribe out this morning, so yeah, more next week. This’ll suffice as a decent precursor to it. Apologies if it’s a bit haphazard \- I don’t know what took over me. I just re-read it and find it pretty boring and rather obvious if you’re observant enough. Hmph.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Retain your value. See you next week.
