# Issue 228

Hello\! If my friends were being generous, they’d call me price sensitive. I’ll be the first to admit I’m pretty cheap. My frugality was born out of necessity thanks to working in indie film, but being budget-minded is a feature not a bug (I repeat to myself… and my wife).  
Sure I’ll show receipts. In NYC, I used to bike to the theater so I wouldn’t be hit with a \$1 online “convenience” fee per ticket. I also used to collect my change and buy a bagel for dinner every other week. I once stooped as low as 2 Bros Pizza for a dollar slice, forgive me father. It’s like my grandad driving an extra mile to save a nickel on the paper. I get it, I think.    
This is all to say that I’m watchful over AMC up-selling The Batman for a couple bucks more per head ([IndieWire](https://www.indiewire.com/2022/03/amc-the-batman-ticket-prices-1234705775/)). Don’t worry, I caught a matinee the first weekend at a Regal to save a twenty. I’m not kidding.  
What’d Wilde say about knowing the price of everything and the value of nothing?  
Entertainment is a non-necessity, so pricing it is some form alchemy – especially now when the consumer can weigh the cost of a \$15/mo streamer for a lifetime’s worth of content, or a \$15 ticket for hopefully a good night at the theater. The promise of an experience.  
There’s all that jazz around supply and demand \-- the market dictates the price, so whatever the super majority of the audience is willing to pay will become the go-to standard. Market rates apply… but those rates can be sorted into inferior, normal, and luxury goods.  
And the cost of making those goods doesn’t really factor into that pricing decision, it’s perception. You’d think it’d be less expensive to watch a Sundance darling rather than a superhero spectacle. But I guess if it were to be underpriced, audiences will think it’s broken… no it’s probably just depressing.  
And the front row of the theater should be dirt cheap. Stadium seating variables. I’ve written about [dynamic pricing back in issue 63](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-63-11-26-18-e598c8fb0d07), and it’s worth noting that studios make more money on opening weekends with sliding scale economics. Rather them than the exhibitors (theater chains).  
I guess there’s the urge to see it early, time frame FOMO, but that’s now literally gone out the window because audiences know The Batman will be on HBO Max in less than a month. And the streamers will continue to price hike, but some have far more utility than others…. well, just Amazon deliveries.  
I’m off to go study the consumer price index and get depressed.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Account for inflation. See ya next week.
