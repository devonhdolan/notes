# Issue 22

Hello\! Last week I got some flak for writing about my disdain for most commercials and that industry at large, so this one I want to present some solutions to help agencies deconstruct the brand narrative to tell a decent story. In its simplest form, stories can be boiled down to two things: the world the characters live in, and the characters themselves. The characters are always the most important aspect of the story. They’re the drivers of the audiences’ feelings and journeys. For instance, Avatar focused too much on world-building, and not the subjects inside of it. Can you name a single character from Avatar off-hand? Didn’t think so. To draw a parallel, advertisers tend to focus too much on the product, and not the message. The best way to communicate a message is through character. They’re like conveyor systems. They’re what create identifiable, relatable, and impactful messages for the consumer. In my opinion, the most successful brand campaigns use characters as tools to exemplify their brands, and in turn, to pedal their product. Think of the Duracell Bunny, Dos Equis’ Most Interesting Man, Tony the Tiger, and this might be a stretch, but Old Spice’s Terry Crews character. These characters create memories, which help the consumer retain information to possibly influence their future actions. What more could a brand desire? The most genius move of all time was done by Wheaties, who utilized their box as the billboard featuring the athlete du jour. So simple. Brilliant. To quickly segue into the newsletter, I highly encourage you all to check out Tren’s writings on Netflix in the ‘read’ bullet point at the end of this email ([25IQ](https://25iq.com/2018/02/10/business-lessons-from-reed-hastings-netflix-part-2/)). Also, we lost a musical genius over the weekend, the Icelandic composer Jóhann Jóhannsson (Sicario, Arrival, Blade Runner ’49) died at the age of 48\. He was by far my favorite composer in recent history, and I teared up yesterday listening to [Flight From The City](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AlftMNmDH00) in memoriam. See you next week.
