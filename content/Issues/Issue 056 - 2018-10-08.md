# Issue 56

Hello\! Late last year, I tweeted “autonomous vehicles are going to be miniature planetariums.” With that prediction, I’ve been observing what people do on their commutes when I take the subway, which isn’t that often because I bike everywhere. The subway isn’t the optimal location to conduct this experiment because WiFi and network connectivity are garbage underground. In the past, people read books and newspapers on the subway. ([Proof](http://i.imgur.com/SoG7Yqh.jpg)) Now, the majority of people twiddle away on their smartphones. I’ve noticed that people still read the news or books, but now they also play mobile games, draft texts and emails, listen to music or podcasts, or watch film/TV. (Yes, I’m that annoying guy peering over your shoulder at your phone. Sorry. Go buy a privacy filter.) Currently, these actions, and this media, tend to be tailored for offline viewing or download-for-later. But what I’m most interested in is what people will do in their cars when they don’t have to drive. Will people work or play? Will our cars have unique, personal identifiers? A digitized VIN that maps to your personal info (Waze)? Will there be new media formats purpose-built for this experience? I think so. Projections, interactive glass overlays, phone screen casting etc. There’s a firm in Zurich called WayRay ([TC](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/18/wayray-raises-80m-at-a-500m-valuation-led-by-porsche-for-its-holographic-ar-display-tech/)) that makes modular, holographic heads-up display technology. I’m watching Niantic, the makers of Pokémon Go, to see if they partner with a car company. In-car entertainment; not too dissimilar from in-flight entertainment. I think AVs will be small too — smart, Smart Cars. When AVs become commonplace, our cities are surely going to evolve. They’ll become smart as well. We will witness the digital transformation of municipalities. (Wrote about this in April, where I focused on the company [Intersection](https://www.intersection.com/)) I’m curious to see if this will play out at the local, state, or national government levels. Likely all of them. In August, I wrote about how OOH ads will become the standard, the first domino to fall, and that’s happening as we speak. The last two weeks, Outfront digitized their billboards in the subway system. Don’t let the future pass you by. That’s all for now. See you next week.
