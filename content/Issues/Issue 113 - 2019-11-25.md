# Issue 113

Hello\! Woah, to be compensated for something you put work into is truly gratifying. Not that I haven’t before, but this time, it’s different. I got my first money sub. It’s a total stranger, and frankly, I don’t know where you came from as I don’t really market this thing. Thank you \- you know who you are \- there was just one last week. I jumped for joy and had a drink to celebrate the occasion, and, uhm, it might’ve been 2pm on a Wednesday. I’ll remember your name for a long time. Appreciate you.  
Something to anticipate (I hope) \- I plan to blow the lid off of gamified entertainment in a longer public piece that should come out in a month or so. I’m going to elaborate on “design systems for content” that I’ve touched on here over the years. My first article of this nature, which was co-authored with my good pal Michael, outlined a [framework for immersive storytelling](https://techcrunch.com/2016/01/14/redefining-the-axiom-of-story-the-vr-and-360-video-complex/). It was published nearly four years ago by TechCrunch. That article did more for my career than my college degree. No joke.  
I’ve been pitched a couple of interactive concepts that are just screen takeovers. I think this format is bottom shelf. Annapurna’s Telling Lies just does not cut it for me. I have to play detective all day on my phone and laptop already, please don’t make me do the same on anyone else’s. But hey, my throwaways could be another’s treasure. To that end, interactive is so much broader than choice-based entertainment that Eko pedals. Choose your own adventure has been niche, and I think it’ll remain that way. A few more hot takes. I really don’t think many people will want to wear HMDs or AR glasses. VR has been, and will be, better for simulations rather than stories.  
I watched a few episodes of The Mandalorian. I had higher hopes, but it’s still very entertaining. I dislike that Herzog is in it. A distraction. That role should’ve gone to someone else, someone who cares. In [this brief interview](https://variety.com/2019/tv/news/werner-herzog-the-mandalorian-star-wars-jon-favreau-disney-plus-1203400810/) he says he hasn’t seen any of Favreau’s other work, didn’t inquire how Mando was made, and hasn’t watched any Star Wars at all \- what a nutter. I find it cute that Favreau likely owns an interest in Baby Yoda. His holidays are looking extra green. I wrote about character rights and derivative attachments as an ulterior kicker in [issue 104](https://mailchi.mp/2c3b0ad04764/temeeco-issue-104) since SVOD platforms buyout the backend. These deals are well-shrouded in secrecy.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
