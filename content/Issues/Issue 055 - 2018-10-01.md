# Issue 55

Hello\! I had an adventurous weekend out east with friends — the key to enjoying NYC is to get out of it every so often. I met a couple of brilliant designers and architects one evening, and we talked about how all sorts art-forms can be integrated into commercial and residential real estate. There’s been a boom of modern luxury developments in the Hamptons, and modern homes call for modern appliances. So I got to talking about holographic display companies like [Vntana](https://vntana.com/) and [Looking Glass](https://lookingglassfactory.com/), and all-encompassing curved/domed screens — Buckminster Fuller style (this is being developed right now by Vulcan). There’s not a lot of content for holographic hardware or “stretched” projection formats, so I took that as a signal to riff about the prospects of digital art. The state of the art. It’s safe to assume that most Hamptons homeowners also own extravagant art collections, and that these collections are largely traditional — paintings and sculptures. \[For traditional art to be considered valuable it needs be scarce, non-fungible, beautiful (subjective), and the artist needs to be “pedigreed” and reputable. Digital art needs all those same characteristics as well, I still find Cryptokitties silly. Pricing art as an acquirable asset is a riddle to me — willing buyer, willing seller — which is why I’d encourage collectors to support living artists through commissioned work. I digress…\] Digital art presents a wild opportunity here, and it knows no bounds, except for the confines of the “screen” it lives in. I’m slightly obsessed with data visualization because it’s constantly living and evolving, though that’s data dependent. One great example of this work would be my friend Salik Syed. Here is a piece he made of [Manhattan’s Energy Usage](https://vimeo.com/226044938). (I think I’ve shared this before here, like I said, slightly obsessed.) Two companies that offer data visualization tools are [LiveStories](https://www.livestories.com/) and [MapBox](https://www.mapbox.com/). I also recommend the book [The Visual Display of Quantitative Information](https://www.amazon.com/Visual-Display-Quantitative-Information/dp/1930824130). As you can see, this medium tends to be 3-dimensional, yet the art is broadcast onto flat 2D surfaces and screens. The world is 3-dimensional, and it needs more displays that allow for 3D art to live to its full potential. That’s all for now, see you next week\!
