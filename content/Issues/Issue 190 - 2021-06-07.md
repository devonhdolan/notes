# Issue 190

​​Hello\! I’ve been thinking about media diets and how you can cook a habit into your audience. Time, scheduled. Programmed. Owned. The morning paper, the 7 o’clock news. Beyond habits, there’s potential to instill behaviors into your audience, some form of trusted *interaction*, especially online. Conversion or transaction. The NYT crossword puzzle, a single-player game. Answers gifted tomorrow. A relationship, rewarded.  
I can take a step back and gauge how people consume these emails. The majority are opened within 24 hours. I send them on Mondays, the start of the work week. Deliver on your promises. Consistency. This has been told to me second hand (I can’t see the data, just opens/clicks \- I try to ignore those too), but some readers save it for the weekend, some skip my preamble, others dodge the links. Some start from the bottom and work their way up. None of this is for me to decide, that’d be impudent. It’s your choice. Your time. Not mine.  
But I could probably add some more fun and games to these things, a call and response. Like a questionnaire where I’d share the answers. Polls and dynamic widgets will become the norm in most media-forms. Activity, expected. Insights, collected. But will it impact or control the story, the event, or will it remain on tracks. On rails. Guided. Same can be said about emails. And texts. You ultimately train your audience, the receiver. Output. A shot clock to respond. Read receipts. We are always on.  
Tangentially, a good chunk of entertainment is interest-driven. Now more than ever, the audience can grow into a community, socially integrate. Environmentally, mutually. They can do it on their own accord with Facebook Groups, Discord, Circle, Slack, Disqus, and more. Streamlined access with a QR code or quick-link. Gather round a shared space. This can be in-person and/or online. Schedule events, talks, meetups, and more \- consistently. Don’t follow, join. Don’t subscribe, play.  
Not one thousand true fans, the council of five hundred.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. We’re all students of behavior. See you next week.
