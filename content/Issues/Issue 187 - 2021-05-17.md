# Issue 187

Hello\! I don’t know what’s in the water, but I’ve been inundated the last three weeks with producers asking me to build financial models for their new digs \- from content funds to subscription offerings. I take the meet because they might come in handy later, but more often than not I say no thanks to the ask. Especially after a string of quick qualifying questions, which I’ll elaborate below.  
As flattered as I am, I wouldn’t consider myself a numbers guy. But if you can quick-recall elementary arithmetic and read an income statement, then creative execs will liken you to some sort of banker. It’s a concern. Abacus lost.  
One thing’s for certain, there are plenty more efficient spreadsheet jockeys who live in Excel that probably have templates at the ready for the newco, whereas I’d start from scratch. That’s not to say I couldn’t do it, it’d just take me a hot minute and it’s not my idea of fun (at all).  
To set the stage, most of these producers have been in the business for a decade or two and have fancy awards baring it all on their bookshelves \- that doesn’t do much for me anymore. Jaded. They’ve likely worked on the lot at a talent-fronted pod, or better yet, they’ve been bankrolled by a billionaire. In essence, they’ve lived a fantasy. Non-fiduciary. La la land.  
I usually begin simple, top-down, by asking if they have a slate near greenlight ready and if they’ve soft-circled a few benefactors (I mean investors). It’s surprising how many say no. Sorry, see you later. For those who’ve come more prepared, then I lay out the basics that’ll function as inputs for a cashflow forecast: how many projects you got, what’s your fee estimate on each, and most importantly, when might be they be a go.  
It’s common sense. But it’s a tell-tale sign of who’s made it past the napkin scribbles. Be wary, many are master bluffers, fluffers, and conversationalists \- and the game only gets trickier from there. The truth pill is in the track record, which most potential suitors will jump to first. It can’t be honeyed. If your highly speculative business wasn’t healthy with a bottomless bank account, how will it be now.  
I won’t get deep into it, but every other aspect will make the model grow only more complicated, especially when there’s a discussion around equity investments and profits for multiple types of media formats. Start small and scale up in success.  
To round this thing off, you can torture a spreadsheet to say whatever the hell you want. At the earliest of stages, they exist to justify a hopeful vision, and rarely (if ever) do they represent reality. Be rational, to a degree.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. It’s all make believe. See you next week.
