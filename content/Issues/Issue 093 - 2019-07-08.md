# Issue 93

Hello\! We’re so lucky to have experienced this transition, this epoch, that marks the last 20 years in media. The advent and adoption of the web acted as the catalyst that changed everything. The paradigm has surely been subverted, but it mirrors history. There’s a semblance of cyclicality to it — the machinations of the media are programmatic. The walled gardens that were once reserved for the media elite, the privileged, have been crumbling since the dawn of web 2.0 in ’99. We’re now post-empire. Tech usurped the media. It really struck me this weekend when I dug into my archive of magazines that ran rife with once untouchable celebrities and athletes. Those who graced the pages of our ordinary lives must now grease them through self-production and promotion. It’s 2019, every single person and company must double as a digital media company to serve as their own publication, to share their life and interests, to be purveyors of perceived truisms. To be picture perfect. Many of those magazines have since ceased operations. They couldn’t make the jump. I dwell on this frequently. Audience migration and information dissemination. Aggregation theory. I think about how “new” media companies are built off the back of social platforms because that’s where the audience lives, but the platform maintains the power and remains the ultimate victor. Those who grew too reliant on them failed. I believe the next iteration of media companies will circumvent this trend. These companies will steer away from the big tech oligopoly to build their own platform. They’ll use direct channels of distribution to reach their end audience, such as emails and podcasts. The content might start niche, it’ll be consistently scheduled and likely delivered via text or voice. I think there’s a lot of opportunity in speech synthesis software (voice to text and vice versa), but even more in machine translation to open up new markets and engage with broader audiences. The radio and the magazine with some added bells and whistles. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
