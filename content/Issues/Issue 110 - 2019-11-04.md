# Issue 110

Hello\! A few observations. 1\) The racket of digital video metrics: Media companies, particularly sports-focused ones, will front-load their video on social with an interesting clip to juice their “view” count. Smart. Something tangential has been happening on YouTube for a few years now, where creators will stretch their video a hair over the 10 minute mark to maximize revenue. It allows creators to insert more than one ad into the video. I expect to see some sort of digital video “hack” guide soon with more of this type of info. 2\) Invisible, default recommendations: It’s pretty obvious that tech companies favor their own products and shows over their competitors. Who wouldn’t? I have a Fire TV stick — Jack Ryan is obnoxiously front and center every time I go to watch some telly. That first slot makes for some valuable real estate. Same goes for Amazon’s private labels crap on their e-comm site. Curious how it looks on Apple TV for their new content. Right now, Netflix really wants me to watch their film, The King. On a related note, thanks to Search, Google Podcasts is going to have a meteoric rise. Go ahead and type in your fave pod in Google. It pops up first in Google Podcasts. This won’t be good news for the other platforms (Apple, Spotify, Stitcher, Breaker, etc). Just in case you’re wondering, I generally order the lists below by what piques my interest the most. At least the first couple of lines, then it gets jumbled. You sure bet my close friend’s work is near the top. 3\) OTT Audio: There are no clear victors here. Maybe YouTube. I often use my TV as a speaker. Most of the podcast and music platforms don’t have their own OTT apps. Some do, like Spotify, but the basicness is bewildering. There’s a blatant absence of video, like Spotify’s OTT app doesn’t incorporate the looping Canvas visuals that only A-list musicians can access and employ (for now). This would make for some fun background entertainment at house parties. The bigger podcast creators upload the audio (and some accompanying video) on YouTube. See Joe Rogan, Sam Harris, Howard Stern. Someone will dominate this space in due time, let’s be real, it’ll likely just be Spotify. Combining aspects of points 2 and 3 here, but I’d bet that Apple, Amazon, and Google will revamp their smart TV efforts soon. They already have the dongles and smart speakers with virtual assistants. They’d probably collect all of your viewing habits to inform their content decisions too. 4\) Security. Last one. Just some friendly advice, don’t use the same password across all of your logins, and please don’t store them all in your Notes app. If someone gets your password, the dominoes will fall. Most people get “hacked” through social engineering. And if your computer or phone is stolen, anyone can reveal your saved passwords by adding a bit of code when you inspect elements. Always exercise caution. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
