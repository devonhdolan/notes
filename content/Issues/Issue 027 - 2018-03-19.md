# Issue 27

Hello\! I was a bit salty this weekend so I decided to vent by ranting about advertisements. They just sets me off so easily. Not just the storytelling components, but the industry in general. What a racket. I was initially triggered by Scott Galloway’s video ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1PA0qg-TVZY&t=1s)) that touched upon influencer marketing, and then Charles Hudson’s tweet ([link](https://twitter.com/chudson/status/974490648026923008)) about startups obsessing over customer metrics like CAC and LTC (this is a bit more sensical to me though). I think ad metrics and units are mythical, and that some sorcerer convinced companies they need to spend money on ad schemes like pay-per-click and cost-per-thousand-impressions. These just seem so synthetic and nebulous. Going back to Charles’s tweet, this got me focused on the early stage CPG companies that raise gobs of venture then blow a portion of it on subway ads. I’m looking at you Quip, Casper, and Allbirds. Sure, I understand that this is a brand building exercise to try to create relational brand awareness, but if you’re going to do that, at least have a discount code that maps back to a specific subway line. I believe that ads should enhance the bottom line and be used to measure a company’s transactional audience base by geography. Marketers and business execs in China seemed to have found a solution here in the form of QR codes. They are nearly ubiquitous, and actually augment the out-of-home advertising market. Speaking of, I hope that AR will integrate with QR to bolster this industry because they will allow ads to be a fun, mobile consumer experience, while also being “shoppable” and “followable.” Another solve is to just make a product or a piece of content that people actually want to seek out, which spreads via word of mouth virality. Easier said than done, I know. Suffice to say, I don’t think I’ll be invited to Cannes Lions anytime soon. See you next week.
