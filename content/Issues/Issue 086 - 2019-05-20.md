# Issue 86

Hello\! I’ve been back for exactly one month, and I finally feel like I have some semblance of a routine again. I’ve taken a hard look at how I budget my time as I’m now personally accountable for my productivity — one throat to choke. I need to find an app that helps me stay on track. Many early-stage founders and filmmakers have told me they face a similar problem. Any who, today I want to spotlight the creatives that are often over-looked and under-appreciated in the entertainment industry. VFX artists and sound engineers are lightyears ahead of their time. These groups are relegated to lightless rooms and left to their own devices to make magic, literally. It’s sad to think that most producers and execs have no idea what these technicians are doing. Hell, they’re eschewed as nerds on the daily. They’re the real heroes (along with the writers). The marketing department gets a bit more love, but not enough. They have some of the most unseen power as well. I’ll never forget when I pitched a project to a friend at a studio. They liked it and said they needed more eyes on it. They then submitted it to marketing, who killed my dreams. Marketing must have a pulse on the cultural conversation and be abreast of the changing media landscape to target audiences on a per-project basis. That’s a tough task. I’m convinced the entertainment industry is stacked with the most genius marketing minds. Last week I got a little heat for my remarks about tech twitter. I think they need a better outlet to channel and award their self-praise. That’s partially why Louis B. Mayer created the Academy in the late 20s (well that, and to combat unionization). Again, genius. To round things off, I’ve been toying with the idea of how to structure a co-fi slate partnership with an exclusive SVOD platform, where the content lives there in perpetuity. I’ve been digesting how Spotify defines royalties (songs have the repeatability factor), and am trying to unearth Shonda’s and Murphy’s “profit participation” deals at Netflix. Most FAANG companies (and Disney) don’t need outside money though, excluding Netflix bondholders. That’s all for now. If you’re in NYC, check out NYU’s ITP spring showcase this week. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
