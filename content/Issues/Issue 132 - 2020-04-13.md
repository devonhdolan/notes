# Issue 132

Hello\! Hope you’re all keeping safe and sane. We’ve gone a tad stir crazy, but who hasn’t? I was pretty disappointed in my preamble last week. I’ve said it once or twice before, but I put a fair amount of pressure on myself to muster up something relatively new, meaningful, and insightful in each post, while avoiding heavily talked about subjects (Quibi, Covid). It can take a toll, but more-so, it can feel like a big distraction. A burden. Especially when Sunday afternoon rolls around. The quickest way to lose interest in keeping something sprightly, alive, is having it become a chore. And I really don’t want these morphing into snarky rants \- I’ve had my share. Here’s another:  
I’m going to keep these light(er) the next couple of weeks. I’d rather do one or two “features” a month that can actually make some noise as a standalone piece, than drivel out half-baked diary entries. They might drive traffic, erm, money, to this newsletter. I welcome any ideas. I have two wiki-hole type articles planned. Both focus on pioneering game companies from the mid-2000s that don’t exist anymore, but the founders have gone on to build some really cool shit.  
Last week I promised some thoughts on ageism in Hollywood. I don’t want this to be construed as an *it ain’t fair* babble, but it’ll probably be taken that way. Oh well. A few things went down that make this slightly more topical. Paramount and the Killers of the Flower Moon producers are in the hunt for tech money (a Netflix or Apple window) to get the \$200m film made, and [SNL parodied Masterclass ads](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VN2p8D6Mawc&list=PLS_gQd8UB-hKbruovFC53YET_ApBG1zjf&index=8) pretending to be Chalamet and more. I died. It’s a constant barrage of them on YouTube.  
Flower Moon’s a hot property. The book-to-film rights sold for \$5m. A bidding war with all the AAA list players. That’s a rarity. I’m excited for the movie, don’t get me wrong. Scorsese, Leo, De Niro are attached. Yes please. And it got me thinking. De Niro and Scorsese are both pushing 80\. They’re absolute treasures, and they’re old AF. The industry rolls over whenever they want to make a movie. It’s because the decision makers, studio execs, were wildly influenced by their landmark works. Hell it’s probably why they’re in the business in the first place. Goes for me. I signed up for Taxi Driver (1976), Goodfellas (1990), not The Irishman. Not Silence. But what person in their right mind is going to turn down these grandpas? Not me.  
Marty’s got a Masterclass. Other heavyweights include Aaron Sorkin (58), Ron Howard (66), Anna Wintour (70), David Lynch (74), and Werner Herzog (77). Their “lessons” don’t really apply in today’s age. The landscape is totally different. Yes, all the above are kings are queens. Timing and luck play such a huge factor in creative fields. But they’re old school titans that haven’t heard “No” in decades. Masterclass pays these celebrities upwards of \$100k to make a video, and they’re offered 30% rev share of it ([2017 THR article](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/what-masterclass-online-courses-pay-lure-hollywood-stars-as-teachers-993159)). Hollywood people are mercenaries. They’ll take the bird in the hand. And well, they probably see this as a form of charity too. Especially when their namesake and experiences carry so much weight. But they’re fundamentally not teachers. It’s just this antiquated industrial complex \- the fat cats get fatter.  
From the CEO/Co-founder of Masterclass, “Our lecturer felt it was his responsibility to “send the elevator down” for the next generation of actors. It sometimes feels like the impulse to teach is an integral part of being a master \-- they love seeing the work of the next generation.” Get paid along the way \- [here’s *that* scene from Donnie Darko](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cgt_kJQdjes&feature=youtu.be).  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
