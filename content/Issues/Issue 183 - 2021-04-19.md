# Issue 183

Hello\! So I don’t really have anything to rant about this morning. Perhaps I’ve found inner peace, enlightenment. No, acceptance. I swear I’m okay. These emails have been essays the last month or two, so a shorter one is a nice bit of welcome relief.  
I’ve been reading (and writing) for pleasure recently. Crazy, I know. It’s a form of escapism, an active experience, where you build out the world. It’s a muscle. Imagination. In film and tv, the audience is spoon-fed the vision. It washes over you, a wave. Inspiration. And social media is mind poison, death. Distraction.  
I’ve been putting the pieces together on a few creative (and business) pitches lately. They’re an amalgam of other ideas, where I pick, parch, and stitch them into something new. A woven tapestry.  
And in doing this, I kind of felt like a fraud as I referenced dozens of other works: articles, books, movies, music, startups, and more. It got me thinking about the monologue from Jurassic Park where the [ever-steamy Goldblum goes off at the creator](https://youtu.be/4PLvdmifDSk?t=60) about standing on the shoulders of geniuses, which ends in the infamous line “your scientists were so preoccupied with whether or not they could, they never stopped to think if they should.” But that’s where the best ideas come from \- everywhere.  
Nothing is original. Remix, culture. “All creative work is derivative.” \- Nina Paley.  
Speaking of Jurassic Park, I firmly believe Crichton, Roddenberry, Lucas, Ridley Scott, Asimov, Gibson, Jim Cameron ([etc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Pop_Culture#Science_Fiction_and_Fantasy_Hall_of_Fame)) have collectively inspired more scientists, technologists, and engineers than damn near anybody else, certainly more than any dense documentary or scholarly work. Piece of advice, try to not get caught measuring yourself to those you’re stealing from (like an artist). Comparison, it is the thief of joy.  
As you might know, my mission is to produce STEM-centered stories for any medium (elevated sci-fi), and scale irrationally early-stage media tech startups, to do just that \- hopefully inspire the next generation of scientific creators. Yes, yes, very altruistic. It’s also a very fun process.  
I bring this up because someone once told me there was a state-backed study in China about sci-fi, where the government interviewed their top scientists, and they thanked Star Trek, 2001, Star Wars, Blade Runner for leading them into their field. Ultimately, China went on to fund a program designated for sci-fi creators around this same agenda.  
I’ve gone to the fringes of the internet and can’t seem to find the study, and I dare say, I’m a very good internet detective. If you stumble across it, please share avec moi, that’d be so kind. I will happily give you a shout and toss in next week’s opener (if you want). It supports my whole thesis.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Make do with what you’ve got. See you next week.
