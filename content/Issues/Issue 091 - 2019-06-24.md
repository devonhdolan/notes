# Issue 91

Hello\! I really enjoy hanging with friends while queuing up music videos on the telly. It provides for a fun, conversational atmosphere. Vibey. I think music videos, surf/skate films, and commercials (to an extent) are great discovery tools for creatives, while many of my pretentious film/tv friends often overlook (or more aptly: eschew) these directors to place emphasis on the “real” filmmakers who struggle for 2–10 years schlepping around a short film or two on the festival circuit — more often than not, those filmmakers come from means. The Cannes Lions just happened, and I’d go on a whim to say that Hollywood largely ignored it. On the flip side, junior managers flock to short film programs en masse, climbing over each other, to rep “the next big thing.” This puzzles me — success is often hidden in plain sight, just go where the wind isn’t blowing. The debate of the optimal calling card for creatives is real. It’s totally uncertain, bespoke for the individual. There’s no one script to follow. I appreciate it when filmmakers go big with the inexpensive feature right off the bat, at least there’s a chance for upside. Shorts that range from 10–25 mins are an almost equal slog, and there’s pretty much zero market for them. A couple of my friends were catapulted into the ad world by making unofficial music videos and commercials in film school. That could prove to be a better strategy than making a short for your thesis. (A thought on this space. Many music video directors and producers aren’t entitled to net proceeds. This should be fixed. Vevo and the record labels are eating up all that tasty ad-share.) All of this is to say, I’m eager to see what might be the next platform for creatives: podcasters, Twitter / Reddit threads, TikTok memers, video game streamers. Who knows. We live in a mobile first world, so why not pursue mobile first storytellers. This is the new mainstream. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
