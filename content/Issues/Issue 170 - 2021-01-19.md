# Issue 170

Hello\! Let’s start with a quick gift \- here’s a [Dropbox link](https://www.dropbox.com/s/tmu6opkw6quryoj/facts_on_pacts_2021.pdf?dl=0) with recent Facts on Pacts. I didn’t make it (don’t know who did, Variety used to publish these annually) but it’s pretty comprehensive \- also, looks like Kinberg might ink an overall with Apple. That’ll be huge. Enjoy some consolidated industry info.  
A quick edit on last week’s note, which [elaborated my feelings](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-169-1-11-20-11e319e651f8?source=friends_link&sk=9897633c917d11fda05996d3c7438f86) on what went down at the Capitol and the ensuing events. I goofed and opened it with the Mad as Hell monologue link (you know the reference), but it directed to a Deadline article, which happens sometimes when you copy/paste like a hundred links an issue. [So here it is](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MRuS3dxKK9U). Embarrassing, I really beat myself up over the small stuff. Appreciate all the kind words I received, I was on eggshells after hitting send. Self-censorship.  
Okay, now back to our regularly scheduled programming. I had a shower thought the other day that I thought was neat, which would be easily accomplishable with a VPN and/or cheap distributed int’l labor \- all you need is a Netflix subscription. A group of people could manually scrape Netflix data and assemble a breakdown of Tops 10s by territory. You could take screenshots of titles and see which actors and assets perform best with computer vision, and label it of course so that the database is sortable. This would answer the age old question, who has the most clickable face? It might help a development executives with casting. And it’s wild how Netflix will surface trending actors even if they have a minor role, when Queen’s Gambit was all the rage they showcased that Anya Taylor-Joy was in Peaky Blinders as the main tile. Associative properties.  
Next. I think marketers, especially those working in consumer entertainment, need to think like experience designers. Stunt orchestrators. A trojan horse campaign. If you’re just buying ad space online and elsewhere, you’re going to have a bad time. I literally don’t know how to separate from the static… by making memes I guess. I still can’t believe that the cranberry juice skateboarder\* is still able to milk his 15 seconds of fame, talk about legs. A friend said record labels are asking (read: paying) him to plug songs and it’s apparently working. If you’re peddling any product, I hear it’s best to opt for a consortium of micro-influencers rather than one big fish. Always monitor conversion, that’s all that matters at the end of the day. Impressions mean nothing, income is everything. My two cents. It’s a game of luck.  
Lastly. I’ve been thinking a lot about how hard it is to name things \- companies, films, products, etc. I’ve grown slightly tired, dissatisfied, with what I named my company… and it was only a year and a half ago when it came to me. I ranted about picking up [domains and socials in issue 89](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-89-6-10-19-2f73fcf25566). All the good, simple names are taken. And if you’re in film/tv, the principal of the company is the first that springs to mind when you mention a prod co, so why not just name it after yourself (narcissistic yes, but who cares). It’s a bit different with studios as they’re faceless. This newsletter was called something else at one point. Your personal brand should be tied to your name, and hell, it might die with you. That’s your legacy. What a positive note to end on.  
A day late because it was MLK Day yesterday, an important holiday back in the states.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Be kind, love yourself. See you next week.
