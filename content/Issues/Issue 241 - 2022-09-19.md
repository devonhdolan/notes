# Issue 241

Hello\! The concept of being a “multi-platform” *value’s* driven production company is omnipresent, yet alludes. Stand for something. For those who don’t, nothingness begets pollution. Or worse, oxygen thievery. Satisfactory prevails in the content age, for both consumers and creators.  
Let’s rejoice in strong first principles. Simple really, what do you care about, and why.  
Build your brand around it, beyond just matters of taste.. everyone thinks they have it.  
When most people think of *value*, they jump to a financial metric – that’s the sole motive. We’ve designated it as such. But it should mean so much more, the *soul* motive (sorry).  
In an idealized ‘post-capital’ society, value capture/creation happens across many through-lines: cultural, social, emotional, ethical, aesthetical, reputational, relational, educational, spiritual, political, organizational, familial, ideological, environmental, communal… I can go on.  
In the arts & culture *industry*, we tend to view projects through the lens of their commercial, critical, and/or creative appeals… a project’s potentiality. Or by the vanity of its perpetrator(s); which is just industry itself. The latter triumphs. We are subjected to their (our) sinful whims and desires.  
Your credits are your currency. Notional value.  
Frankly, after taking a few steps away from dutifully servicing the industry on the daily, the commercial aspects of it are staring me down in grimace – the returns & terms of today’s film/tv business are dogshit across the board; and when you assess viewership data, at least what’s publicly available anyway, the numbers are bleak.  
Value(s) be looking like red candlesticks; paint the town with ‘em.  
All-time lows, for another year in a row.  
Fade to black.  
I can get more cynical, but that’d be woefully unattractive. And honestly, this news is rather bracing and motivating. The tides are turning, there’s so much opportunity to be had. All the best stories are yet to be told.  
Martyrs are lost to the mayhem, the madness… “chaos is a ladder”  
A dawn of reciprocal media.  
Reverberate resonance. The 90-9-1 Rule ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1%25_rule)): 90% are lurkers, 9% contributors, 1% creators. If you can make a guild out of that last 10%, you’re golden (my goal for NewCo). This likely throttles out at Dunbar’s Number ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbar%27s_number)), the amount of active relationships one can maintain (\~150).  
This rang too true the last few issues when I said I was giving away some digital objects that held some sense of monetary value, and that I’m developing a *grants*\-based fantasy writer \+ worlder fellowship. I’ve been blatantly begging for replies since my return in [issue 236\.](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-236) And the 90/9/1 numbers have been almost exact. Maybe it’s just desperate experimentation.  
The 1% are the whales. The addicts. They drive the vast majority (\~80%) of revenue back to game publishers. Pareto Principle ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle)). The vital few. Offset risk with them, early. There are new industries with “healthy” economic activity to do just that. I’m adamantly anti-volatility.  
Now, I only look for scalable, inhabitable, stories where the creators generally want to go direct to community and appeal to their potential whales… and the masses. Chase upside; align incentives, together. Live performance-based entertainment does this quite well, and creatives get a real-time feedback cycle.    
I think the town is going to get fractalized to smithereens. More next week. Agree, disagree? Write me back. Prove the 1% Rule wrong. If you want, you can [join my private Telegram group](https://t.me/+i5axj89FG2oyNDcx). We can talk in there too. I dare you.  
Remember, this is all supposed to be fun :)  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. People need purpose. See ya next week. 9 left.
