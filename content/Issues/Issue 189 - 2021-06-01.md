# Issue 189

Hello\! This one’s a bit scattered, as am I. Need to organize my thoughts, will get to that later. Re-writes. Okay so I’m going to roll around in the mud again about “control and group dynamics”, I did it only three months ago in March in [issue 177](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-177), and have touched on it many time before that \- my fave being [issue 165](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-165).  
Since most of my activities are centered in long-lead group project work, I think about it a lot. Decision-making *powers* (creative and business) and the why. The future we are selling, and to whom. That’s my cornerstone. It’s all sales. And it’s all the time. The equation changes in the journey: from hiring/casting, to fundraising, to selling and presenting the end product (to a studio or at the individual/enterprise level). The dynamic is built like scientific control measures, resulting in a yes or no changed outcome. Win or lose, it’s all games. These spirited *powers* generally boil down to provenance (ownership) and governance (control). Tribune of the plebs.  
We work for the project, the goal is to get it made (w/ aesthetic), and ultimately, the audience owns it \- if you’re lucky enough to see the final leg of its life-cycle: distribution. Think about it. At the end of the day, the end-consumers are the ones paying for it. Watching it. Using it. Spending their time with it\*. The ticket is the perceived value, from an outsider’s viewpoint. Some can’t be bothered to download or read a creative work for free, or respond to a well-crafted email. That hurts. But yeah, the final product is put on display for interpretation. T2: Judgement Day.  
But along the way, the power balance shifts from different parties. Collaborators. Stakeholders. Toll collectors. Group members. Co-founders. Audiences. Whatever you want to call them. This ties to the above (the ‘to whom’ line), and is dependent on those who have the best relationships, that’s where responsibility is grounded. If only we operated in an idea meritocracy. Don’t start with the execution dependent bs, that is uhm, *everything*. Unless you can control and own all aspects of the pipeline, which I generally do here with this newsletter. \*Thank you so much for your time, seriously.  
The project value chain is more or less tethered to Tuckman’s stages of group development ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuckman%27s_stages_of_group_development)) \- forming, storming, norming, performing, and adjourning. There’s a harsh realization here. Two splinters emerge, endless *outbound* abysses, during forming and also between norming and performing. These so-called splinters are near-forever hunts. The packaging phase, it’s self-generated duty. Liken yourself to a lone wolf, though if you’re independent it’s done mostly on spec, so perhaps you’re more sacrificial lamb material. The value of your hour is whatever someone’s willing to pay. Results vary: priceless, worthless.  
Budgeting time. The clock ticks. There are always more emails to write, people to call, scripts to read, lists to assemble, photos to post, ideas to uncover, submissions to make, but where do you draw the line. Responsibility infinite. There is no more clocking in and out in today’s world. The works stays with you, a saddle. Workhorse. I will refrain from writing too much about lead-generation and dropping Glengarry quotes, but know that David Mamet wrote a whole film about *The Leads* ([here are all the quotes in their glory](https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0104348/quotes/?ref_=tt_trv_qu)).  
Prioritizing and scheduling attention. Is it spent or invested. Sweat equity, it doesn’t keep the lights nearly as bright as I’d like — I can say that with authority. The Pareto Principle ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle)) says that 80% of consequences come from 20% of causes. A strategic volume game when it comes to the outbound. The schedule is imbalanced based on priority, with no set time allotment, it is just re-distributed on preference. And time is the only owned resource you’re given off the starting block. It only diminishes. He giveth and taketh away.  
And as I was flipping through the Tuckman wiki, I came across needed character archetypes for each stage. We have to play roles as a group, our collective circus. There’s a ringleader, lion tamers, instructors, acrobats, tent erectors and many more. We all probably look like clowns. Same could be said for any sort of organization, say a sports team or government. One’s a bit more high-performing than the other. Film sets are more dictatorial. People are the problem, and the solution. It’s all fun and games and time. Whatever you’re making, make in enjoyable. For everyone.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading, for your time. It means a lot. See you next week.
