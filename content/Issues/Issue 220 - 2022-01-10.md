# Issue 220

Hello\! I’ve been thinking about the composition of narrative, more so character and perspective, across mediums. In more mainstream work, especially film, I truly believe a main character with purpose is needed to motivate the plot forward… you know, the hero’s journey and all the rest. That’s our central engine driver.  
Racetrack. The audience should ideally see (a version of) themselves reflected in the main cast. A shred of shared ethos attracts us to their stories, where our internal monologues might overlap. Novels can wander and shift between characters, much like television series. Increased running time allows more fluidity, attention drawn out.  
Steering wheel. Most (open-ended) games, and some of the underlying mechanics of web3, put the user at the center of the story. Some have real-world impact, interplay. This *design* adds a new organizing dynamic and allows insight into human behavior, an invisible hand. Though many rely on mystery [ARGs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternate_reality_game) and [LARPers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_action_role-playing_game) to supercharge lore, and for users to situate themselves in a virtual world. Two-sided communications.  
Broken fourth walls. I don’t find rhetorical questions effective in literature, unless they’re written in the first person. Otherwise the writer is invoking the reader to ask themselves something directly about the story, which breaks the illusion of narrative… Like, what do *you* think about that rhetorical questions?  
In-jokes. I must say the self-referential schtick has grown tired. The audience sees the wink. It’s a form of navel gazing that begs for a laugh \- no, acknowledgement, or maybe even kinship. How sad. It’s not clever, it’s actually lazy and condescending. It’s only cute when a man in a red suit (Deadpool) does it… it’s that character’s genetic makeup. The [opening](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s8TJ2d7-1e8) in Bergman’s Persona (1966) does it with masterful artistry, probably because it alienates us, capturing our imagination. Subversion… subtlety is superiority.  
So I’m sifting through these scattered thoughts, but think I’m going to have to soon throw them into some sort of coherent rubric… like how I did in this six year old throwback to a piece I [wrote for TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2016/01/14/redefining-the-axiom-of-story-the-vr-and-360-video-complex/) with a good friend (hi MP) that put a framework to immersive storytelling.  
It’s all rhetoric and dimensionality.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Suspend disbelief. See you next week.
