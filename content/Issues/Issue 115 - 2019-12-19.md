# Issue 115

Hello\! Just a heads up, this is probably my longest issue to date. My preamble alone will take \~4 mins to read. Here it goes:  
When I was younger, I routinely cycled through YouTube-first media companies for content, also known as multi-channel networks (MCNs). I still do, kinda. And early in my career, I prepared one too many research briefs on that same ecosystem. I’ll be a Machinima (RIP) fan-boy for life, and will rave about VEVO whenever I get the chance. It’s a creative collective. A coterie centralized and curated by shared taste and theme. It is the foundation of most every publisher and producer, built off the back of aggregated creatives. Programmed.  
Exactly one year ago, an Instagram-first media company called Roam emerged with partnerships to my favorite adventure photographers (Jimmy Chin, Chris Burkard, Renan Ozturk) and \$2.1m in backing from investors like WndrCo, Advancit, and Susa. [Here is the Insta](https://www.instagram.com/roam/). I finally thought someone was going to capitalize on the chasm that GoPro and Red Bull failed to own in the not-so-niche actions sports space that highlights human potential and achievement. A sort of NatGeo crossed with Surfer’s Journal. Yes please.  
Sadly, Roam hasn’t quite seen widespread success and critical acclaim (yet). I fail to understand why, but I’ve been thinking about it a lot. They have a stacked lineup. The A-Team. Oscar winners\! A coalition of the best of the best when it comes to travel and adventure filmmakers and photographers who have captured some of the most intense moments across surfing, snowboarding, climbing, biking, etc. At first I thought that they failed to fully embrace omni-channel as their YouTube is lackluster. In today’s age every media company needs to be totally cross-platform, even if the content is *subtly* duplicative. Example, a photo (or a short \[automated\] clip) better promote a video edit, just as a a tweet or LinkedIn post promotes a blog or a newsletter, a trailer promotes a film \- and there’s a teaser for that trailer. At the end of it, these all promote the person. The individual. The product.  
Then it hit me. This socialist-style approach to content distribution just might not work now. Those who capture and create these moments have their own brand to build. Why push and share *your audience* elsewhere when you can *own it yourself*. Individualism over socialism. The clout transfer model, more aptly: conversion rates, should impact your income rather than a media company’s follower count, even if you’re an equity participant. How would you like your upside: owned or pooled.  
Aside from the general “collapse” of the MCN market, many floundered because the most successful personalities would just bail to start their own channel. Fun fact, now just 28% of the top YouTube channels by subscriber count are actually YouTubers, the rest are celebrity musicians, talk show hosts, actors etc. [Peep this infographic.](https://twitter.com/TZhongg/status/1203471111305879552/photo/1) Instagram never needed MCNs \- though you could make a case for Overtime. When it comes to attention: videos \> photos. Whatever the case may be, my eyes are still glued on Roam.  
Quick sidebar on Apex Legends, the free-to-play video game that did \~\$100m its first month of launch. They eventize updates of their maps and in-game rewards in *seasons* with the release of short films based on a certain character. They events are usually two weeks long, here is [their latest announcement](https://www.ea.com/games/apex-legends/news/voidwalker-event) and the [accompanying short film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yU_eip8IvQU). Solid stuff. They also involve aspects of the community, like hosting a fan art challenge where the artwork makes its way into films and merch ([EA](https://www.ea.com/games/apex-legends/news/season-one-fanart-challenge-winners)). The crowds seem very pleased, which is next to impossible because gamers are a fickle bunch. [Here is a quick rundown](https://www.criticalhit.net/gaming/apex-legends-voidwalker-event-launches-today-watch-the-incredible-new-lore-filled-cinematic/) of all this from CriticalHit. EA acquired the developer behind Apex, Respawn, for \~\$400m in 2017\. A big thank you to Daniel Angeles for clueing me into this type of transmedia and evergreen content. Brilliant.  
So the Forbes list also came out (as did a bunch of other subjective lists). I played A$AP’s Tony Tone when I flipped through it. [Here is the music video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ulZrXxFaoUY). It’s rad \- delicate ears be warned. Speaking of A$AP and lists, he was my artist of the decade on Spotify Wrapped (which isn’t saying much seeing as how I’ve been on Spotify for only two years \- I had my Pandora dialed). I really do love Spotify’s yearend roundup. [Here is mine](https://twitter.com/devonhdolan/status/1202571013386653697) (it’s a tweet). It’s invigorating because 1\) this is a rare case where content providers actually publish their user data with complimentary visualizations and 2\) people actively share it almost as a status or taste symbol. More-so, they anticipate it. It’s ritualized. If you didn’t share it, then I guess you must live in shame. It’s probably the truest, unfiltered reflection of the individual. The anti-thesis of vainglorious. It’s like asking a friend to show you their Screen Time. Raw. Data.  
Next week I’ll cover some of the differences between Substack and Mailchimp. Oh, and in case you didn’t know, the archive of this newsletter is up on Medium. [Check out my landing page here](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter). Share it, or don’t, though it’d be nice if you did. If you’re a Medium member, I dare you to read the articles that are in the partner program so that I can see some pennies. I did get stiff-armed by them (yet again) as they only allow 3 posts per day \- thankfully they lifted my limit.  
Nothing like reading through posts from two+ years ago to feel like a complete buffoon. Cringe factor was off the charts. It was also my 10 year high school reunion the other week back home. I was stuck here in NYC. A few friends sent me my yearbook photo. Again, cringe. Everything’s changed, and so has nothing. Once a kook, always a kook.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading (this time especially). See you next week.
