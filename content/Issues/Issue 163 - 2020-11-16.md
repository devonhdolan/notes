# Issue 163

Hello\! There’s a peculiar trend I’ve observed that centers in dual-sided reputation transfer. It revolves around this new crop of “leaders” who (perhaps strategically) initially rode the tailcoats of those with successful followings to benefit their own standing \- social capitalization of namesake. Leech-seek. Same applies for companies and titles, and universities. Pedigreed. In short, build a brand off someone else’s, then ditch ‘em once you’ve extracted your pound of flesh, but stay close. The remora grows off the whale, the whale finds satisfaction in minting another.  
Being guilty by association. Springboard, bounce.  
This is a weird sort of conversion game at play. [Presubscribe](https://www.presubscribe.me/leaderboard) is a dolled-up version of it, and perhaps the [Readwise leaderboard](https://readwise.io/twitter_leaderboard). Funnily enough, these two instances are generally confined to the ghetto that is tech Twitter for “business-types.” But it happens all over the internet. Hype houses are of a similar strain \- get a bunch of influencers together and share in follower-ships. One could argue that celeb-driven studio first-looks aren’t too dissimilar here. Jesus had twelve disciples, right?  
Next. I’m irked by games and experiences that offer interactivity, but the choices are meaningless. The story continues the same no matter your input. They’re on-rails, pseudo-responsive. [No reward](https://medium.com/hovercast/how-interactive-live-streaming-is-removing-the-barrier-between-audience-and-show-a29ec89ccfd8). If you’re crafting an experiences of this sort, might as well produce a film or write a book. Though perhaps the touch-points are there to keep the audience awake. Not to point fingers, but the recent Apple Arcade game South of the Circle ([CNET](https://www.cnet.com/news/apple-arcade-south-of-the-circle-adventure-game/)) is designed as such. I didn’t finish it, so it might’ve changed in the later innings. There are many, *many* more examples in the wild.  
To be fair, I’ve been dreaming of creating an interactive livestream event \- a play in a sense \- that offers choices to the audiences that would prompt pre-planned, timed cutaways at decision points. It would react to the individual’s participation (answer) and not adhere to the whims of the crowd. Personalized. This idea is challenging. I think all the scenes would have to be produced day-of so that it feels *truly* live AND interactive. Some audience members would be able to see it through uninterrupted. This gives a whole new level of meaning and pressure to actors to “hit your marks.”  
A tangent because it came up a few times last week \- at what point does Netflix start licensing their originals? They’re spinning up a linear channel and I imagine they’ll sell ads against their programming schedule. I don’t think House of Cards is quite the customer trap it once was… the cow begs to be milked, and the industry will do so. I’m sure Netflix is putting formulas and trackers to the performance of their films and tv shows “off-network” which means there should be new income spilling in and profit potential for the creative team (but no). I wouldn’t want to be tasked with monitoring the accounting frameworks here.  
To the best of my knowledge, these deals haven’t been struck. And this idea brings me back to the territorial top-10 list bonus that I [wrote about back in April](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-133). Some big dogs really need to go toe-to-toe with Netflix on these points, but if they’re in a competitive situation with multiple buyers they likely won’t rock the *precedents* of the Netflix boat because we will all undoubtedly be pitching them for quite some time. I don’t think anyone will *really* be able to change their ways. Bad habits never die.  
Something I’ve been asked out here a couple of times and am still formulating an answer \- how will you benefit NZ industry? I’ve never asked anything of the sort to foreigners back in America, and if I did I’d be rightfully put on blast. Any who, I’m undergoing surgery, a re-calibration, on the professional front. In short, I’ll be spending more time writing (more than these newsletters). Let’s just keep it at that.  
“Look, the only reason we have these kind of conversations is because of the lack of imagination on behalf of the people who have behaviorally modified the audience’s expectations,” Fincher.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Eh biens, continuons. See you next week.
