# Issue 114

Hello\! I read too many words without action, and sometimes, I write them too. Hoover said these are the assassins of idealism. Guilty as charged. These grandiose proclamations, predictions, ideals, miscalls \- whatever you want to call them \- grind my gears as they’re often not put to the test by the wannabe pundit. Walk the walk. A few that come to mind are around remote work, AR glasses, and growth over profits. Let’s jump in.  
I’ve been working from home the last six months. It is extraordinarily lonely. Humans are social animals. Even the introverts, the lurkers, crave connection. Everybody who I’ve spoken to about this, from solo founders to filmmakers and freelancers, sing this same song. It’s why so many people whip out their laptops at the local coffee shop. In-person camaraderie can’t be beat. Friendship. I think a day or two working from home is idyllic. Chop up the workweek. Hey, it’s why work from home Wednesdays exists.  
AR glasses. This one’s easy. I wear prescription glasses and will do almost anything to not be burdened by them. So I toss in contacts, which aren’t ideal either. I’ll get laser eye surgery when I can afford it. This is for my sense of vision. *To see*. AR glasses are a feature, not a requisite. In my opinion, the chief use case for AR glass is for data display, yet sometimes I prefer to live in the dark. It’s why I don’t want to use the email service Superhman, which has pixel tracking to clock open rates. Don’t get me wrong, I’m a huge propagator of AR tech. I just think that if you’re putting hardware on your face, the occasion will be more situational for when it’s needed.  
My take here is probably the most uninformed because I’ve only worked at small-ish companies where cash is king and I’ll be the first to tell you I’m no finance wizard. I’m not going to break ground in a sentence or two, but operating in the red for 10+ years just doesn’t make sense to me. I get reinvesting free cash flow to scale a business (Amazon), and raising capital to build a product and/or capture market share, but overall \- the burn rates seem too damn high. Bleeding for the sake of it should cause death. Later stage hyper-growth companies like WeWork, Lyft, and Uber aren’t helping matters, they’re causing a hullabaloo. Yes, there’ll always be a lot of money to be made in this game. Maybe I’ll revisit this subject at a later time. It’s a snooze.  
All of this reminds me of that magical Good Will Hunting scene where Robin Williams puts Matt Damon on blast. It is definitely one of the most powerful monologues of all time. “So if I asked you about art, you’d give me the skinny on every art book ever written, but I bet you can’t tell me what it smells like in the Sistine Chapel…” [Here it is](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dEIQSbul9Os) if you want to watch it. It’s too good.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
