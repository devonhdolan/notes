# Issue 54

Hello\! First off, I just need to say that I had a very inspiring weekend. I went to an MC Escher exhibit (think, Inception) and the Space and Science Festival on the Intrepid. I also watched The Terminator (the OG) and October Sky. So, my brain was buzzing with ideas. On the Intrepid, I was over-the-moon (ha) when I got to ask a senior NASA JPL engineer about alternative launch techniques with lasers ([StarShot](https://breakthroughinitiatives.org/initiative/3)) and catapults ([SpinLaunch](http://www.spinlaunch.com/)). He chuckled and said, “There are many ways to skin a cat, as long as everybody’s working toward the same goal, to advance mankind.” Then he talked about g-force and I pretended to understand what he was saying. Anyway, some of my favorite conversation starters are: what are you most excited about, what motivates you, and what’s your media diet profile. Let’s mull over the last one for a minute. Do you think you have a healthy, balanced media diet? What is the content, how do you get it, and how long do you consume it? In the simplest way possible, I tend to think there are two types of media, news and entertainment, and that they’re built to grab your attention to (ideally) communicate a message. To state the obvious, there’s been a monumental paradigm shift in how we consume and/or interact with media thanks to the advent of social networks, which I believe have encouraged us to favor shorter media lengths because of accessibility (read: phone). Snackable content. Quick hits. Vine six second videos anyone? I’m very curious to know the neuroscience (cause-effect relationships) between different types, sizes, and formats of media on an individual and societal level. Is it a net positive or negative? Best case scenario, it actively inspires and advances humankind. That’s all for now, see you next week\!
