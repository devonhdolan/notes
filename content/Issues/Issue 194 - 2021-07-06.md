# Issue 194

Hello\! Given the holiday weekend back stateside, I had a "*free*" day to stare at the clouds to sip on what I think it means to be reckless and accountable, a harsh deviation from last week’s note about leadership… and kindness. Propagate it, do it with no question \- reference check.  
I’ve found there’s some absence of fiduciary duty of those in positions of power, especially those who can’t *afford* it. It boils down to how (and where) people spend their time, voice, and most importantly — money. All things everybody hopes to expand, but usually contracts. Except for the *fortunate* few.  
Simply, I’m concerned with frivolous spending. Always have been, always needed to be. Like a church mouse that eagle-eyes the expense column. Budget balanced. It seems now that people don’t mind operating in a sea of red. Death by a thousand cuts. Or \$12 green juices. Outflows. This counters everything I’ve been taught. Responsibility disregarded, reckless.  
It’s on the mind because there are some newfound cost savings around fully remote, or hybrid work environments. Redistribute those savings, or hold it to extend runway. Fancy offices ain’t cheap. Hell, there’s a content company here that hasn’t shipped a blockbuster, but is hemorrhaging cash for their sweeping corner office views. Lifestyle creep. They could’ve easily bought a block and building a mile away for the cash equivalent. Boggles the mind. Perhaps it was justified for employee happiness, well-being, but most sit in a darkened bullpen. Mmk. There’s also a film/tv company in an *elephant*\-sized space on Santa Monica Blvd that hasn’t released a project (?) in its near half-decade existence. Big yikes.  
Another culprit here is the domain name, likely a first purchase for that new business idea. I tend to get over-excited and scoop up multiple domains at once so people don’t squat on adjacent names, usually around the descriptive qualifiers (i.e. ventures, films, studios, partners, labs, games, media, productions, etc). Here’s a hint, nobody will. That’s like forcing people to sign NDAs before they hear a pitch. Concern yourself with making, and saving, money. The boat sinks.  
Yes we all want to own that little slice of the internet, that .com (the first NFT), but that does not prove the product. And you’re going to over-value and obsess over it. I’m guilty here. It’s a seller’s market. Financial motivations abound. I know a few founders who could’ve paid *multiple* employees the cash and stock equivalent for what they paid for their domain (and social handles), and that’s before launch. Big yikes.  
Many of the most successful people and firms have the most basic of landing pages. Hell, most people in film/tv don’t even have a website. It’s on purpose. I found [this procurement story](https://twitter.com/gaganbiyani/status/1375137698483036163) rather alarming for the Maven name, a new online education platform. Maven is also the name of a production company, that’s what I think of first anyway.  
Here’s a novel idea in today’s age, income should be greater than the expenses. Bear fruit, penny pinch. But here we are, perhaps I’m the charlatan. The greater fool theory in moving color. You should be proud to report to your backers and partners (and friends), who in turn will report to theirs. It’s turtles all the way down honey. And if that idea goes belly up, those “assets” will be the first to go in the event of a firesale. Liquidated. Wasted.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Be mindfully frugal. See you next week.
