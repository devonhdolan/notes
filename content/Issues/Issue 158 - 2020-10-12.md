# Issue 158

Hello\! Well I’m out of isolation and am slowly assimilating back into society. The last 6+ months I’ve trained myself to mask-up, social distance, and avoid all human contact. The “new normal” is the old status quo here in NZ, and I’m the alien that needs to normalize… to un-wire my brain and settle into our new digs. We’re very happy. The peace of mind is real. And thanks to all for the kind bday wishes :)  
Last week’s [note](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-157-10-5-20-5bcfdf50d7d7?source=friends_link&sk=e36c71da324398150e51da59c0ddd990) was absurdly long. I wrote about Range and a slew of other related topics, and frankly I should’ve broken it into two segments. But I didn’t. And in the top of that email, I alluded to self-reflection and existential dread in the dregs of quar. That hasn’t subsided much, and I don’t think it ever will. But here it goes.  
Over the summer \- about a year into starting my newco \- I came to the realization that I was emulating (and chasing) successful people “on the business side” because for a period of my life there, I found it shrouded in myth and wholly unattainable. And so I did what any person would do once it was within reach, I made friends with some of those figureheads and learned as much as I could to fill in my educational stop gaps.  
It was (still is) a highly rewarding process because when the world opens up to you, it also shrinks, and work becomes a playground. A Lego set. But, you need a defensible “way-in” to approach the industry because it is just that, an industry, and it’s heartily transactional. Best believe that people keep tally, a little black book, of all their good deeds. They don’t go unpunished. Status, game.  
For the longest time, I thought that your occupation was the *way-in*, and for many, it is. It’s how careerists see themselves. But it’s just a safety net masquerading as an entry point, a cute rubber stamp, that might get you into the party, but you gotta show up to dance. Wallflowers ultimately wither on the vine. You’ll be defined by what you do and how you do it, and once that’s set in stone, shedding the label is not as metamorphic as you might think. A calculated re-invention. For many of you, I’m probably just “the newsletter guy” and that kills me inside. Identity, meet crisis.  
Rather than obsessing over the business of it all \- uhm, these newsletters speak volumes about my interests there \- I should’ve been following in the footsteps of say Favreau or Soderbergh. They’re serial storytellers, experimenters, who embrace new technology and business models that break the antiquated constraints of the legacy empire. The old guard. They invent new molds, fashions, for production. That’s what I’m all about. At the end of the day, the industry gravitates toward creatives. They’re the beacons of taste with credible names that can actually get things made, over the line. The best instruments of persuasion. All you need is one “yes.”  
To finish rather abruptly as I know I’ve lost the thread, I was obsessing over the wrong things at the right time. And that’s okay. The work will inevitably speak for itself. Money talks, bullshit walks. And for a little dose of pop-philosophy, when people introduce you to others do they lead with a verb (your personality) or a noun (your job), your tastes and interests, or most importantly, what you’ve done. Your epithet is your epitaph. The ends will justify the means. I hope.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
