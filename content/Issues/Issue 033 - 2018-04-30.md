# Issue 33

Hello\! Tribeca is over and I feel like I can breathe again. I saw one film for pleasure, and went to the VR Arcade to try to watch some immersive content. Cinetic was repping ten films at the festival, my favorite of which was [General Magic](https://www.tribecafilm.com/filmguide/general-magic-2018). The GM team was way ahead of their time having built the first smartphone. The film I watched for fun was a doc called [Momentum Generation](https://www.tribecafilm.com/filmguide/momentum-generation-2018), which was directed by the Zimbalist brothers. They profiled the celebrated crop of American surfers that emerged in the 90s, such as Kelly Slater, Rob Machado, Shane Dorian, Pat O, and filmmaker Taylor Steele. The story is about friendship, competition, and integrity. The subject matter is near and dear to me as I grew up surfing everyday, and some of the guys featured in it helped me get my first sponsors, namely Pat O. He’s the king of Salt Creek — my local spot. Kelly Slater is the GOAT — [here](https://imgur.com/a/LzRLOsw) is a pic of his homescreen that I grabbed off his IG story a few month’s back (nerdy, I know). How’s the crypto folder and Candy Crush in the dock?\! I recommend the doc. It’s fun. On the other hand, the VR Arcade was a different experience for me. I went there on Saturday for the 3:30pm track and waited 90 mins (halfway into the three hour session) before I got notified for my first viewing (of something I didn’t care to see but registered for to bide my time). By that time, 5pm, I had left the building. How it works is that the festival opens its doors to its VR section three times a day, and there’s this Black Friday rush to hit the iPads to sign up for specific exhibitions. Then, you’re expected to twiddle your thumbs until you’re called upon as next in line. The dreaded “hurry up and wait.” It’s a broken system, and happens at most festivals. I’m lucky to not have paid for my ticket, otherwise I’d be pretty upset. To round this opener off, the consensus is that many of you enjoy it when I’m a bit more polarizing, which is great because it’s easy to write about what I like and dislike. (The advertising piece has been brought up to me on multiple occasions.) With that said, I’ve hinted at my contempt of MoviePass for long enough, and am writing a Dan Loeb-esque letter to them. Standby for it next week. Ciao\!
