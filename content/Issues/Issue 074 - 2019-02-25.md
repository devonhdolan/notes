# Issue 74

Hello\! Or should I say, aloha. I’m crashing my parent’s vacay in Kauai for the week. We’re going to get some sizable surf in a few days, like 15–20 ft. Amped. Okay, I’ve been thinking a lot about AI again, but through the lens of linguistics. I’m quite proud of [issue 53](https://mailchi.mp/c51e2c034655/temeeco-issue-53) where I sought to categorize AI into three modes: prompt-based, statistical, and generative. I believe that, at its core, AI is about using a dataset to achieve a specified outcome through computation, ideally real-time. There are two key words that come to mind in this process, reduction and extrapolation, and the interplay of the two, dialectics. These are concepts most common in statistics, where they’re used to reduce random variables in a study and to forecast trends from known information. Reduction informs extrapolation. What makes them most powerful, and us human, is the ability to use these understandings in production to infer and convey meaning. I like to relate this notion to film editing, specifically [Soviet montage theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_montage_theory), which is the comparison of two distinct images (one static, one changing) that results in new feedback or context for the audience. This is basic arithmetic (Fixed X \+ Dynamic Y \= Changed Z). From there, we go into application. More later as my thoughts clear. I’ve hardly scratched the surface. Finally, big kudos to my Cinetic fam on the wins last night, we were fortunate to play a part in both Free Solo and Green Book. So special, surreal. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
