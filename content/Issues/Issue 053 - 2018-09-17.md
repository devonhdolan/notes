# Issue 53

Hello\! I saw Childish Gambino on Saturday at MSG. He is raw talent. The performance was downright amazing. What a show. [Here](http://www.childishgambino.com/) are the dates and locales for the rest of the tour. Go\! The NatGeo documentary Science Fair is in theaters now. It’s incredibly motivating and entertaining, and gives us hope for the future. It’s about brilliant high school students competing to get invited to and win Intel’s ISEF. It led to 500+ patents last year\! [Find](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/films/science-fair/?) a theater near you and bring an impressionable kid. I recommend it. One charming and curious student who dons Hawaiian tees stood out to me in particular. He barely graduated high school and didn’t get invited to any of the universities where he applied. But through his obsession with creative AI, whereby he built a neural network to write and perform a Kanye West song, he was invited to intern at Nvidia directly out of school. Here is his [GitHub](https://robbiebarrat.github.io/index.html). I love that story (there are many similar ones in the doc). To that end, I’ve been thinking a lot about AI recently. You can’t have AI without IA — information architecture (complemented with big data). \[It’s important to note that diversity of background and thought is important in AI, otherwise there might be implicit bias in the machine learning models.\] I think that you can group AI into three buckets: prompt-based, statistical (predictive/reductive/recursive), and generative (creative). The first is centered around recognizable commands and questions. We see it now in Alexa, Viv, Siri, and Google. Personal AI. The second is algorithm-based. I’ve found that predictive AI crunches data into pre-built forecast models, such as a linear/logarithmic regressions or Monte Carlo simulations. I’ve met a few “script analysis” companies that say they can map sentiment in story and therefore predict a movie’s success, but it’s not reliable, at all. Blasphemous… for now. The third is the one I find most interesting because it’s in active R\&D — unexplored territory. It’s spoken about in hushed whispers by my smart technical friends. I tend to think of it as being self-generative and interactive, and it being applied to the humanities and creative fields. This is where we get into discerning taste patterns and mirroring human cognition. It’s automated and might end up becoming improvisational (scary). Straight out of sci-fi. To me, this is all fun to contemplate. It leads me to ask questions like: What’s the nature of intelligence? Will AI eat the software industry? If yes, how? Is creative AI even necessary? Why? What are the implications of it? The ethics? Let me know your thoughts on this subject — you can reply directly to this email. Thanks, and see you next week\!
