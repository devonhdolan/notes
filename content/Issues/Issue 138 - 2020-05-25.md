# Issue 138

Hello\! I think these times are going to birth a new movement. A system. Young Turks. In the middle of the last crisis, the sharing economy experienced substantial tailwinds, which created more seamless paradigms for the gig / on-demand economy to, you know, satisfy user *demands*. This empowered people to carve out their own little markets in an attempt to be “superhosts” or “superdrivers.” Localized fiefdoms. The billion dollar startups tackled massive industries \- hospitality (Airbnb) and transportation (Uber). Frankly, the business models and the deployed technology weren’t *extraordinarily* groundbreaking. Marketplaces, baked in community, mobilized. For many individuals, the side hustle morphed to the main hustle. Business was conducted over the internet. Online. Mobile. It’s all certainly much more nuanced than what I’m presenting here, but those are some opportunistic thoughts baked down to a few core ingredients. Entrée.  
The recession heated the pan, founders buttered it. And we’ve got a totally new meal to cook. Predictions are meaningless unless pursued. Progress. Profit.  
The work-life re-shuffle is barreling toward us head-on. The way we work, how and when we do, will be redefined. U.S. unemployment numbers are on par with the Depression era. Stocks are up. The government is printing trillions. Nothing makes sense. I’m not an analyst. Nor a journalist. I’m just a guy trying to stay online, trying to make ends meet. Provide the essentials: food and shelter, and maybe a beer here and there \- you charming paid subscribers afford me that luxury, so thank you.  
It seems that the future of my domain, the whole entertainment economy, is in flux. Such a silly specialized “vertical.” The industry*, the system,* has remained relatively the same for the last few decades, though new techniques and models have certainly challenged and usurped the incumbents. Netflix eats the cake. Virtual productions are going to become more commonplace. History rewards those who move forward in the midst of chaos. The past does not repeat itself, but it rhymes. Embrace that \~ fortune favors the bold \~ attitude. We got this. Stay productive, positive : )  
I’ve severed ties with a few commitments that were going to be major time-sucks to free up more bandwidth, RAM in the noggin, to start this new thing. To run at full(er) capacity. High alert. The microfund is more or less on ice. More for another day. The economics aren’t that great, the market is pretty saturated (no matter how specific you get), and you’re just one small peg in a giant machine ordained to service the actual doers. Get rich first, and then just angel invest. Go build. The racket perpetuates itself.  
I’m trying to “solve” something that’s born out of my own experiences over the last decade or so. I find it wildly invigorating. It’s not even that out of the box, though it is, if you catch my drift. Parts of this wheel have been invented, they just haven’t been assembled in standardized fashion. After three weeks of market research and potential customer interviews, I’ve decided to take the leap. It begins with the domain and social media handle herding. Always a pain, I’ve complained at length about it before. G Suite admin and website host integrations. Slack, Notion, and Airtable. Death by 1,000 cuts; someone’s gotta bundle it (likely Google). I spend my waking hours toggling between like 8 different emails and just as many group hubs. It’s psycho. I find Typeforms or Forms helpful for managing inbound and building community. Oh yeah, and most importantly, a deck and a plan. I need to get back to all that fun stuff.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading (and for being my collective therapist). See you next week.
