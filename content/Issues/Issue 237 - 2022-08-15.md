# Issue 237

Hello\! When everyone is entertainer, there is no encore. All the world’s a stage, but nothing lands. A gesture-less, invisible audience. Please clap; deafening silence. Static radio feedback. This is the now, the dehumanization of “social media,” the numb commodification of likes and follows.  
The [para-social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasocial_interaction) networks are polluted, empty states of vessel and receptacle. Our shared microplastic trash heap. An algorithmically charged ecosystem of super-drains. Spun around in tangled web.  
So let me tell you how I really feel.  
We crave affirmation, affection, I certainly do. It’s hardwired in our behavior, seek compassion. Today’s media platforms reward consistency, we are its programmable workforce. This estranged societal shift toward the attention economy is riddled with faux-engagement.  
The medium, no \-- the metric is the movement. Impressions are a made-up concept so the advertising duopoly can continue to part brands from their cash reserves. All in the sacred name of distribution, consumption. The loss of the chronological timeline, of friends’ posts, was our fall from grace. Trending content plays to crowds of couch zombies, tube-fed mind poison, to be converted. Productivity wails from the grave.  
Prince [nailed it](https://twitter.com/devonhdolan/status/1557859134204424192) when he said, “don’t let the computer *use you*… y’all saw The Matrix.”  
User, turn giver.  
Our dynamic with media must become more relational, reciprocal.  
Here’s to theater, live performance, in all its purity. And tales told ‘round the campfire, over drinks. Standup. Where one-to-one-(to-many?) connection is made. Where there’s serenade. Brian Koppelman (Billions showrunner) and Liev Schreiber (the Ray Donovan) [discussed aspects of this earlier this year](https://open.spotify.com/episode/0uM5Ir7AFalfvQE1twkmC7). A dose of therapy.  
To be seen is to be loved.  
Let’s aspire to *give* real thanks. A heartfelt DM. A round of applause. A scattered hoot and holler. My favorite is when people cheer at the end of the movie \-- it makes sense at a festival or premiere when cast/crew are present. Or to the pilots on a plane, steered by machine. Here’s to landing safely, and for the complimentary in-flight entertainment (and beverages).  
This happens online occasionally, wherever the public flock to review and rank — one of the last instances of bringing a creator/consumer relationship full circle, that births anew with the critique itself. Letterboxd. Rotten Tomatoes. I read these *after* I’ve watch a movie, don’t want others’ biases to infect my own. Word of mouth wins, from trusted sources.  
Interactive design systems will evolve with incentive architecture. Get fully aligned, togetherness. A creative, communicative collective.  
On that note, I’ve been listening to Quentin and Roger’s [Video Archive pod](https://open.spotify.com/episode/6cXR6828nYr4CivZByMLon?si=fzETFI6kTfacX1zPNJPy8Q&nd=1). I’m bringing back the movie club [issue 196](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-196) – where we enjoyed classics like Videodrome, Shampoo, Touki Bouki & more. Conversations will be held on Zoom/Discord (reply directly to this email if you want access). We’re watching [Dark Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Star_\(film\)) next (it’s on [YT](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jZUhSqgBEAI)), by John Carpenter and Dan O’Bannon. Absolute legends.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Double-Tap-Like. See ya next week.
