# Issue 164

Hello\! So, I guess I’ll elaborate what I’ve been telling all my close collaborators, my co-conspirators, the last few weeks about why I’m dedicating more of my time to writing *stories*. Let me state this plainly, I’m not abandoning any of my current activities… I’m just being far more deliberate with my time and outreach. This little diatribe here is just me thinking aloud which should help me articulate myself more clearly and concisely in the future, I hope.  
In case you’re unaware, about a year ago I started my own shop to produce STEM-centered stories for any medium and scale irrationally early stage media-tech startups. (This newsletter is just a fun side project, btw) Things have been going quite well, but it goes without saying, this year has been absolutely bonkers.  
At the start of it I was living in NYC prepping to go on a roadshow to raise a micro-fund predicated on a few of my successes flying solo \- proof points. I’ve since tabled the idea of managing money (for now) and it’s partly due to the factors I’ll outline below. I am still riding high on a couple of those verbal commits, thank you. I’ll save why I convinced myself off the idea for next week (and more), which will be a sort of second installment to today’s note \- my reasoning.  
In short, it was a fanciful diversion, and not at all what I set out for myself in the industry about a decade ago. Fast forward to present day, I’m living in beautiful New Zealand and the winter doldrums are in sight back stateside. It’ll be summer here, and you’ll find us 1\) caring for the family (our purpose for being here) and 2\) romping around beaches in our vintage Land Cruiser built for overland expeditions. Psyched.  
To cut to the chase, a few *super* basic principles have emerged, crystalized, on how I should orient myself around story/startup projects as a lone wolf. Weighing risk reward \- my sanity and freedom \- to focus my efforts (attention) as it’s easy to get distracted by the shiny play-toy.  
So here’s my assessment sheet, or rubric, as every project comes with the pressure of pretty much defying impossible (i.e. not failing)… a piece of cake, it is not.  
In no particular order:

* Ownership \- do you own the outcome, the idea, the company (if so, how much\*)  
* Control \- business and creative; who answers to who, the one with more ownership generally has true decision-making powers in the event of a tiebreak  
* Time Horizon / Schedule \- when might this “be a go” and therefore generate income (sooner the better, duh)  
* Achievability \- is this even feasible under current circumstances, what’s the direct path to production, nimble resources are ideal  
* Organization Structure / Project Support (Group, Team, Individual) \- waiting/relying on others (talent, capital, distribution) is death and breeds uncertainty  
* Pay-Off, Return, Upside (\*Financial, Societal, Reputational, etc.) \- a huge plus if it’s stable, consistent, predictable, and recurring

If I were to draw it out, it’d be a geometric prism in the shape of pyramid… an octahedron (I think) and each pillar shifts with a different perspective. The touch-points more or less exist on sliders, tipping scales. An *interlinked* mental model as each notion influences the other. Damn that was jargon-y. Sorry.  
At the end of the day, it takes an army to get a project off the ground, and it usually boils down to the credibility of the creator and cast/team to make the project more attractive, *undeniable*, for that elusive YES. The idea industry generally falls back on these not so lucrative get-made-get-paid scenarios. It’s an uphill battle no matter how you cut it. A tough business, and you got to be in it for the love of it. I love it. So much.  
Further explanation will be next week, this one has gone on long enough.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Be worth your while. See you next week.
