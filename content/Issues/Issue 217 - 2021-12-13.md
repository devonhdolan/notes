# Issue 217

​​Hello\! Beyond food and shelter, there is (positive) acknowledgement. It’s base level for how we fundamentally interact. We crave reciprocity, in the physical and digital. This can be structured and packaged back to us as engagement layers, game design systems, and/or feedback loops. Simply, communications.  
In its essence, it is *recognition*. And it is our move beyond the attention economy.  
The call merits a response, a relationship accepted. Actual dual-sidedness between creator and audience creates loyalty. These are one-to-one, one-to-many, or many-to-many. The long-tail interests and rewards for the parties can be aligned (and perhaps verifiable at the point of sale) to be emotional, societal, fiscal, reputational, and/or cultural. Aim big…. for all of ‘em, or wherever you place your importance, influence, incentive. Whatever.  
The fall of the one night stand.  
I’ve been thinking about how this might apply to the ticketing industry (movie, concert, museum etc). Increasingly, I think it’s becoming ever more important to get out of the damn house, to unplug. Recreation is entertainment as escapism. To witness an experience is to create a memory. Reap the benefits, from the small screen to the silver one. Or just enjoy the beach or park. The world’s our stage. Self-gratify.  
Attending a show earlier in the opening weekends is better for all true stakeholders as it ideally boosts word-of-mouth, and because the studio (and therefore the creatives) will capture more favorable economics — the exhibitor/distributor split works on a sliding scale. Exhibitors are movie theater chains, distributors are studios.  
Day & Date. The theatrical release window collapses. We self-cannibalize to online-only, resulting in a terminal migration to a slew of subscription services.  
The industry has changed enormously the last few years, and consumption habits are scattered all over the map. It’d be nice if there was some sort of nod to the dutiful fans who still show up at the theater… unlocks.  
A few haphazard ideas off the top: ability to resell tickets, special social media filters, raffle entry for collectible memorabilia (physical/digital — scripts, props, storyboards, BTS, OST), referral codes to earn redeemable points, discounted downloadable link / merch, shoutouts etc.  
The dynamic should be holistic, evergreen, to create virtuous matrimony — to the film/franchise, to the studio itself (I’d argue only one *really* owned its brand in the last decade), and/or the creatives directly.  
As a purist, I’d wager the winning solution won’t be offered by the theaters. Regal has an Unlimited 90 day program that’s reminiscent of MoviePass, and AMC continues to make a joke of itself in meme mania. It could be on the back of the ticketing service since there’s already studio buy-in. Fandango is owned by the Comcast (NBCUniversal) and WarnerMedia/Discovery, and Atom Tickets is backed by a few studios — notably Disney which tends to do a pretty good job at getting people out of the house. Or a startup might emerge to be white-labeled. Who knows, just throwing spaghetti against the wall here.  
The industry needs a few more version updates to get to modern times.  
Next. It’s relieving knowing we’ll soon be slowing down into the holiday doldrums where the town closes its doors for two weeks (this newsletter will likely go dormant for a good week or two as well). It’ll give me some much needed time to do the actual work. People around town unhealthily tie their happiness to how full their calendar looks, and trying to achieve any sense of flow is reserved for the weekends.  
I’m very much building the plane as it’s taking off, making me a full-blown stress ball. Hit one or two unexpected potholes last week on the runway. No turbulence.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. To be seen is to be loved. See ya next week.
