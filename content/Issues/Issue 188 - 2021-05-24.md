# Issue 188

Hello\! I can’t help but notice a sort of re-rise of the educational startup, aka an online learning *community*, that are just exclusive content companies with message boards. Curriculum, paywalled. I’m just spelling out some quick, dirt simple observations \- I’m not knocking the space, I actually think it’s an admirable one. The mirror in the classroom.  
So a flurry of new companies have announced themselves recently that are more or less unbundling Ted Talks and Blackboard, but they’re dedicated to a specific niche and/or media format. The first recognize-able batch that are all about a decade old and wildly successful are Udemy, Coursera, General Assembly, MasterClass, SkillShare etc.  
The new(er) kids on the block are BookClub, Metafy, Maven, Educative, Lambda, Formation etc. The OGs are reinforced here thanks to their heavy advertising spend and in-video sponsored segments on YouTube, where they seek to siphon off existing viewers looking to upskill. Smart. Heuristic.  
Whether they know it or not (they must), there’s an artful subterfuge to how they’ve amassed their audience, their customer base. They leverage names that have dedicated followings and incentivize the talent with an affiliate link. Pray for conversion. It’s cost effective. Circular. Bring in a name (a teacher, coach, icon), pay them a healthy fee, record them in a basic studio space, and exploit their brand / knowledge. It applies to the fitness industry. And the church. And the kitchen. Whatever’s your temple.  
There are some drifters in the middle like Gumroad and Teachable that give creators the tools to stand up their own storefront. I like this approach, it’s a bit more laissez faire. As soon as you start “signing” people, acrimony will emerge. I pretend to not care, but I feel this way about Medium and Substack paying writers and starting *their* *own* media destinations \- imagine if YouTube did that. A rift between platform and publisher. Playing favorites, teacher’s pet.  
Generation home-school. I’m not too sure if this new crop is as venture scalable as some hope to believe. It makes me think there could be a dedicated content fund at the trial and error stage, as they’re starting a media company in and of themselves. To this end, it seems that *every* major company and brand is doing just that, launching their own media division \- hell, they could probably find one of them to sponsor or underwrite their first series of lessons. Sounds like a good use of marketing dollars. Brand-supported. Thank me in the credits. Those who can, do.  
I find that a lot of what’s coming out of these big-brand media arms are subjective, skewed articles and eventized content \- like seminars and open panels. Aspects of the press tour, unwound. They should host classes instead. Might as well peddle some merch while you’re at it. It’s en vogue, but certainly not fashionable.  
I think it’d be neat if some of the newcos were to bolt on a talent marketplace to *access* industry experts, job opportunities, and current affairs. Empower the student. Don’t do the traditional thing where teachers are incentivized to jam their pupils on updated editions of their self-authored textbooks at university. Disgusting.  
Any who, this is all top of mind because we’ve been having car troubles recently and the sages who post tutorials and how-tos on YouTube and Internet forums (FB Groups) are life savers. Soon, I think vehicle manufacturers and/or auto repair shops will just own this space completely \- like, here are your complimentary troubleshooting videos. Tips and tricks. Or better yet, here’s a one-year course for mechanic school. When that happens, at least I’ll trust it a bit more as there’ll be some added legitimacy. Right now I’m placing a lot of faith in some random, backwoods engine fixer on the Internet.  
Though there’s beauty in that.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Get learnt. See you next week.
