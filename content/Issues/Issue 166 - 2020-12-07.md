# Issue 166

Hello\! Well, well, well. Ann Sarnoff and Jason Kilar really did it. For the uninitiated, I’m talking about the heads of Warner Bros and WarnerMedia who decided to drop all their 2021 releases on HBO Max the same day the films are released in theater ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2020/12/hbo-max-warner-bros-movies-deal-jason-kilar-explains-move-1234649871/)). Dune, Matrix 4, Reminiscence, Space Jam 2, Godzilla vs Kong, King Richard, and many more. Easily over a billion dollars worth of the goods. A bet on streaming… an investment. The church bells are ringing. Funeral toll.  
This is good for audiences and bad for the industry (especially exhibitors \- movie chains). It’s a sign of the times. The since-cancelled Kevin Spacey called this reckoning back in 2013 in Edinburgh ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/entertainment-arts-23802572)). I don’t think Warners “bought out” the backend, box office bumps, or first dollar gross on any of their talent or co-financier deals. Many people are upset \- I doubt this will go down without a fight. Though I’d say this is not jumping the shark… HBO Max just became a must-have service for everyone \- stateside, that is. They’re employing a rolling thunder release strategy meaning few will churn off as they’re eventizing releases every few weeks and will own our collective dialogue. Hell, it might even be financially lucrative \- for them, and them alone. A huge move that I suspect will ultimately pay off.  
Funny though, I heard that a few of the biggest names in town signed landmark deals with Warners (instead of say, going to Apple) as there was the promise of theatrical releases. I’m not so sure if that can be trusted. They did seemingly stand by Chris Nolan’s Tenet. I’ll give them that. So it now looks like Universal might be the newly favored prodigal son. My friends at HBO Max have some work to do, like perhaps open it up to the rest of the world (hello from NZ) and get 4K geared up. Tick tock goes the clock.  
I’m phoning the rest of this one in with re-posts. I’m proud of my last two newsletters and got heaps of kind feedback. Appreciate it. Here they are in case you missed them: [Part I](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-164-11-23-20-26f831b0e0c9?source=friends_link&sk=860b9ef0e0f5a7aa10c50aea7b3c27b8) and [Part II](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-165-11-30-20-161a70a7a439?source=friends_link&sk=61d0301bdd72d868b8eb90ea1ee36cca). They’re about yours truly, more or less. And below is what wrote last year in [issue 115](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-115) about Spotify Wrapped. The big winners for me this year were Moby and Aphex Twin’s Avril 14th, which is the first song on Spotify’s Hipster Cinema playlist \- embarrassing, I know.  
I really do love Spotify’s year end roundup. [Here is mine](https://twitter.com/devonhdolan/status/1336049880590999552?s=20) (it’s a tweet, \**updated with my 2020 tops*). It’s invigorating because 1\) this is a rare case where content providers actually publish their user data with complimentary visualizations and 2\) people actively share it almost as a status or taste symbol. More-so, they anticipate it. It’s ritualized. If you didn’t share it, then I guess you must live in shame. It’s probably the truest, unfiltered reflection of the individual. The antithesis of vainglorious. It’s like asking a friend to show you their Screen Time. Raw. Data.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Align interests, take fliers. See you next week.
