# Issue 165

Hello\! Today’s note is a continuation of last week’s. If you haven’t read it, [I suggest flipping through it](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-164-11-23-20-26f831b0e0c9?source=friends_link&sk=860b9ef0e0f5a7aa10c50aea7b3c27b8) \- though this could act as a standalone piece too. In short, I say I intend to spend more time writing my own stories in pursuit of making projects I will fully *own*, *control*, and can *produce* myself in the *next year*. I also break down my short list of criteria I use to measure decisions, four of which I just italicized.  
Generate momentum, manufacture wins.  
Let’s lead with it. The last year or so I’ve come to realize that many of my activities were more of a means to an end, rather than my ideal end itself. Don’t get me wrong, I absolutely love what I’m doing. But as a sort of drifter, I’ve dedicated (over-extended) myself to too many projects that are just past the napkin-idea stage. And if they eventually see outsized success (\$100m+ exit), it’d be meaningful for me but not life-changing \- standing a few feet to the side of the real winner. A harsh reality, plus taxes. The founders deserve it \- they’ve put all their eggs in one basket. At least I’m protected by the veil of being diversified… or the idea of it. A house built on sand.  
I left a cookie crumb last week saying I’d give reason as to why I decided to abandon the micro-fund (for now). In short, there are not enough creators (\!), too many VCs, and the economics are pretty shit (remember, I said *micro*\-fund). Truthfully, I see now the same amount of ownership as an individual in each of my respective portfolio companies as I’d see as a ‘first-check investor’ in the carry, without dealing with the headache. And if you’re professionally managing money, your sole purpose is to beat the market. There are far better ways to do that than pre pre-seed venture investing \- gambling done in volume. Startup equity, worthless, until it isn’t. It takes about ten years to be liquid, to weed out the herd, and if I’m part of a runaway hit I think I could protect my share with some SPVs. Oh, and if I’m meeting potential LPs (fund backers), I’d prefer to do that in-person. Money raised ain’t money earned. Bleh.  
And that only touches on the startups I’m advising, the film/tv/experiential projects I’m producing are more time consuming and less lucrative \- especially in this climate. Each project is its own startup. No joke. Generally know that most of my engagements are centered in this ‘eat what you kill’ commission structure. The self-reliant path of most resistance, sprinkled with booby-traps. Prospect for free, return with gold, take a slice (fee) and onto the next. It’s like working a dozen+ part-time jobs. Ill advised.  
“Show me the incentive, and I will show you the outcome,” Charlie Munger.  
In entertainment, along the way of the \- let’s call it the *content value chain* \- there are only like four places (logic gates) where money spills out or trades hand. Transactions: source material, writer hire, financed or/then acquired, net profits (there is no net). And there are plenty of money-grubbing reps (side note: there are good ones) sitting at those junctions with their hands out and pockets open. Roadblocks. Speedbumps. I was one once. Still am to some degree. It’s the way the world spins \- how the industry works. The funnest (toughest) part of the job is in development, which is making the project attractive by punching up story and attaching talent (I’m usually schlepping specs \[completed scripts\]) to then take to buyers or financiers. I skip the first two steps. Collapse the chain when and where possible. You must be an eternal optimist in development because you’re taking it on the chin all day long \- it’s so easy to fall into the disappointment trap. It’s called hell for a reason. The little victory, breakthrough, is getting a NAME attached (takes ages), and that act doesn’t even result in any sort of windfall (yet). Do not pass go, do not collect \$200. Yes, that’s an Ace Ventura quote.  
There’s an important sliver of hope to be captured there. It’s glaringly obvious, but half the time you’re staring down the barrel instead \- icarus syndrome. The way to a financier, or studio / network executive’s heart (wallet) is with named talent, and obviously a great story, written by named talent. Chasing celebrity through taste. Aesthetic. The creators (directors, actors, writers) are the ones with the magnetic pull, where the universe bends more easily to their will. They’re the function that gets the project made, often not the producer \- there are truly only a mighty select few who occupy that throne. I’m serious. Literally, a handful. The producer greases the skids, sometimes they’re a positive beacon, sometimes not. The creators are well-guarded, trapped in precious glass boxes. Do not break in case of emergency. Extricate as many of those people, the naysaying sentinels, as possible from your path. They just get in the way. It’s an outrageous circus. I’m all-in though. It’s a labor of love. I can’t help it.  
So with that, I’ve chosen to embark on my own little writerly mission. I know what’s good, I’m friends with the right people, I’ve got a fresh perspective, and I’ve got ideas for days. I’m ecstatic. I’ll be able to see project through from start to finish \- seed to harvest. I’ve rekindled this newfound sense of motivation, no inspiration, born out of frustration and desperation. There’s no greater calling than this suffix cocktail:  
Vocation.  
Simply, I’m just trying to make cool shit with smart people. Is that too much to ask?  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Do less, earn more. See you next week.
