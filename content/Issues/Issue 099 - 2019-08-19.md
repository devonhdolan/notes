# Issue 99

Hello\! If we spoke this summer, you’ve probably heard me talk about game design systems for content. This is what I’m calling my broad thesis around interactive media, which I’ve redubbed to make it sound more flowery and sophisticated, and to encompass the range of applications that this technology has to offer. When people hear interactive media, they tend to only associate it with choose your own adventure stories but they should be thinking about user experience and interface design as well. There is so much more that we can learn from the audience by introducing new interfaces that consumers can play with, from voice, text, to haptics. This should ideally lead to increased engagement and conversion rates. It seems that most of the online media industry is built on a bed of falsehoods. There are few standards. Views and impressions are each defined differently across the social platforms, built to appease advertisers. Let’s solve this problem. I’ve been concocting new metrics and mechanics for interactive and have thought about them as command prompts: command, input, recognized input, sentiment / feedback tests on input. Rework and recycle. My friend Eli elevated some of my theories here by calling it prompt, participate, react, and reward. He wrote a [landmark piece](https://medium.com/hovercast/how-interactive-live-streaming-is-removing-the-barrier-between-audience-and-show-a29ec89ccfd8) on interactive live streaming that I encourage you to all read and share. I got to play editor on the article and am involved with his software company [Hovercast](https://www.hovercast.com/). That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
