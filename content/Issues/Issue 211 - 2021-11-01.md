# Issue 211

Hello\! I believe the search for self is found through art and history, combined. Our identity is stitched into the fabric of the generations. Before us and after. The days gone by, to be lived. They’re cultural touchstones that collectively capture our historical portrait. Our former-future glory, the times documented, in diary.  
Memory. The rise of our crowd. Of belonging. From an outsider’s perspective.  
My heart broke last night when I gave candy to a lone trick or treater. A young child dressed as a scarecrow. He was around seven years old with a pitched voice full of innocence. I asked if he was with friends, he pointed to his parents on the sidewalk. I smiled and waved, masking my emotions. I gave him a handful of candy and told him family’s all you’ve got. He took the offering in stride. My eyes welled up, my hardened husk shedding. I saw a version of myself in him. He wandered off to the next house.  
Halloween, our costumed reality.  
Escapism. For the last few weeks, I’ve been living in 1980s Vermont with Donna Tartt, Jonathan Lethem, and Bret Easton Ellis. Before literary fame, they all attended Bennington College together. The Brat Pack, a class. The campus as museum, a world to be inhabited. I’ve found kinship in it. The brilliant writer Lili Anolik revisits the era in her podcast [Once Upon a Time](https://open.spotify.com/episode/4ttuaO39jATsMnwEjWz5na) \- it’s fantastic, glam and stylish.  
It’s seemingly in fashion — cultural nostalgia — there’s a sense of longing for it.  
It is bubbling, permeating, under the surface of the now. To be zeitgeist.  
We attach ourselves to whatever we find attractive. Most people group themselves around shared values, ideas, professions, beliefs, and interests. Tastes. Lubricants. Localities. We also gather regionally in neighborhoods, dwellings, forming a community that recognizes each other. Trusts. Events.  
Dopamine rush. A simple good morning, a bartender with “the usual,” or the double-tap-like. With remote work confined to a sliced time zone, our lives can now be more global than ever. Dispersed, diverse. A new form of citizenship is underway that allows for us to explore, accept, and encourage what else is out there.  
Welcome the other. Erase exclusionism.  
A great divide, social media. Our world is an open-closed platform. George Carlin famously said “it’s one big club and we ain’t in it” referring to the upper echelons of society. The walled garden, watered to be burned. Privacy is a luxury that trades in prohibitive prices. It’s overwhelmingly restrictive. The gloss of newness wears thin quickly. Though the combative mentality is wrong: if you’re with us, you’re against us.  
Call it Meta, where misery loves company. The digital realm is now interconnected with our physical lives. They’re interdependent, interoperable. The idea of being online-only is a hallucinatory videodrome. Find your antidote.  
I watched an old Charlie Rose [interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7oo-wNuP9tU) with Donna from ‘92 where they got to talking about [Karl Popper’s Three Worlds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popper%27s_three_worlds): natural/objective, cognitive/subjective, and informative/abstractive. The states of current, concept, and experience — through art and history. A shared definition of culture, hopefully that’s consensus. Togetherness. Knowingness.  
Crack the spine of a new book, leaf through some albums, go to the movies.  
Sit back, relax, and enjoy the show. An audience is belonging.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Let bygones be bygones. See you next week.
