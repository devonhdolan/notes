# Issue 203

Hello\! It’s my last full day in New Zealand. It’s bittersweet. I’ve been here exactly one year. I’m excited to get back to the states where it bleeds eternal optimism. There's a hint of freshness in the air. Spring. The start of something new. Sunshine. A door opening, brightness, rebirth. An awakening. Let’s go.  
Capture inspiration. Boost confidence. Brevity is beauty. Attention spans wane. Seamless design. Aesthetics that please. Create heat. Sales.  
Submissions. I think the best way to tease projects \- concepts generally \- is to visually pack key information in the cleanest, most frictionless way possible. Unless you rely on your company email address to do that for you. That’ll only get you so far, that’s not your reputation (it’s probably your boss’s). You past, best work, is your currency.  
Get to the answer quick. The decision is the point. It’s binary. Yes or no. In or out.  
Websites are decks. Many people will blast out a script with a logline and a sprinkle of references to other films (X meets Y). Yawn. Sometimes there’s a deck, an outline, storyboards, a ripomatic, or some other form of ancillary material — perhaps a written letter or video testimony. A sizzle. Special features. Gimme it all. I’ve only seen it a handful of times, but I’m instantly sold I’ve been sent a link to password-protected site with all the delicious goodies. Yes please, more.  
Obviously this costs some time and money, but it’s persuasion butter.  
Make the work, work for you.  
It’s all marketing. Curiosity hooks. You have mere seconds to grab someone’s interest, and hopefully they’ll toss your script to the top of the queue. Speak to the story, world, idea; sell the product, process, person. The future. I still can’t believe some reps and producers send submission emails as if they’re being faxed with a cover letter. Get with the times.  
Mirroring psychology. The obligation to reply in similar length and fashion. Write short emails, expect short replies. Yes I’m acutely aware I contradict this with these long meandering emails, typed in a fit of fury. And my work website is pathetic, but I’m generally in the outbound business. Each project requires a custom strategy. They’re all different. Bespoke. A bonus in this field.  
Glamor’s lost when everything’s shared. The enigmatic industry works in private. Information games. Secrets.  
Announcements. Press. To me, building in public is a form of exhibitionism. There’s this trend where founders and creators put themselves and their business out on display before really having a product. That kind of openness is an indecency, often misleading. Non-iterative, nor intuitive. There’s no signal in noise. Fame for five seconds, for the follow, drop delayed. The proof is in the pudding.  
Grand ceremony. Publicity, for surprise and suspense. Headlines for self-congratulation. Production companies sites tend to look similarly, or there is none at all. Early-career filmmakers need a reel, same with actors. And perhaps with producers. I like when a company has their own trailer (Imagine, Annapurna). Proposal pre-empted. Hack into the creative industry by making on-brand trailers, commercials, ads, and music videos. Content. Get it in front of the right people.  
Tone. Expose the common thread, the underlying theme. Rarely are there mention of projects in development \- the upcoming slate \- where so often things goes wrong. But this might give reason for people to write with good ideas or intros. Suggestions shared. To stew on later. The hard work is done behind closed doors.  
A couple quick examples. [The Matrix Resurrections *just* launched their site](https://thechoiceisyours.whatisthematrix.com/). Immediate love. It’s my favorite movie, so I’m predisposed. Biased. And I recently stumbled upon this Insta account, [Betty’s Booklist](https://www.instagram.com/bettysbooklist/?hl=en), that summarizes books in the first person. Wouldn’t be surprised if publishers and studios start doing something similar, then content crowdsourced. Feedback loops. Let the audience work for you.  
Curtain call. If you want a masterclass on how the film business works from the highest level, I’d recommend you l[isten to Roger and James Deakins interview the legend Rich Klubeck](https://open.spotify.com/episode/4jx1X1CNcDjxK5rm2VsJ5y) on packaging, finance, and sales. Enjoy.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Less is more. See you next week.
