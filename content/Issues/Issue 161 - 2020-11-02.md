# Issue 161

Hello\! A nostalgic one today. Over the weekend I snooped around a video rental store \- yes they still have those here in NZ \- and it got me thinking about childhood. I remembered running around Blockbuster collecting an assortment of titles while my brother would do the same, then we’d compare possible choices with the family. We could usually decide on what to watch in a few minutes. Everyone would be happy \- a miracle. This is no longer the case. Streamers need to bring back some of those social features… and they should have candy on offer too.  
I should be able to sync lists with friends, even if it’s just the shared accounts on the plan, on the platform and it could pair out what we all favor. And hell, we could even have timed viewing parties. Network effects. This is a trend I’m seeing unfold for a few upstart gaming startups \- well, it *is* the foundation for multiplayer gameplay. It’s a co-op experience (or mission) that builds community around an event, which could also be spun out into a fan base of sorts. Everything’s virtual.  
But no. Now I look to my wife and ask \- what do you feel like \- and it takes us 20mins to settle on something to watch. A ‘trick’ I’ve used to reduce time here is to peruse the “more like this” tab which takes me deeper into the archives. There needs to be an omniscient guide, a clerk, that actually makes sound recommendations based on mood/genre. Now I get a pop up that says literally every movie would be a 98% match for me based on my watch history… yeah okayyy  
While we’re reminiscing about day’s past, let’s eventize the Extra Features section on DVDs. Director commentary, behind-the-scenes, cast & crew interviews \- I’d argue that these are the reasons why people might still *buy* physical or digital copies of movies. Like my friends at [Hovercast](https://www.hovercast.com/)\* (disclaimer: I’m involved) helped produce/direct a tasteful Superbad cast reunion and watch party last week ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2020/10/superbad-cast-reunites-jonah-hill-seth-rogen-for-wisconsin-democrats-1234602818/)). It was a roaring success. Otherwise, it seems that they’re just thrown online, on YouTube, with reckless abandon. No orchestrated campaign.  
To this end, you could probably build a brand, a destination, around hosting these types of BTS activations \- though the studios, trade publications, or filmmakers themselves should ultimately own this domain. I’m always surprised by the sheer number of rather opaque businesses that distribute movie trailers on YouTube. Like there’s this firm called [The BoxOffice Company](https://company.boxoffice.com/) that runs Fresh Movie Trailers, ONE Media Trailers, and dozens of other highly trafficked channels and social accounts globally \- it’s owned by a French holdco Webedia ([wiki](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webedia)). Talk about audience measurement and activation. There are plenty of others that got their start by simply re-posting trailers and have now grown into full-blown digital media companies. Believe it or not, ads are monetize-able…  
Last one, I was trading notes with a friend after seeing Tenet and we came up with a silly idea… because I need to see it again to fully appreciate and understand it. What if your movie ticket had a unique identifier so that you could re-watch the film a second time online (or in theaters) at a discount. It’d surely be abused. And not too many movies have that immediate replay-ability factor. Just a thought.  
“I'm fed up with the idiots... the ever-widening gap between people who know how to make movies and the people who green-light the movies.” \-Sean Connery, r.i.p.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Vote for Pedro. See you next week.
