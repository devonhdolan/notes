**Headline Observations:**  
On every level, more women responded than men. We can all recognize the historical gender wage gap across every industry, so shout out to the ladies for being ultra transparent here.  
On the low end, big red flags to the respondents who reported a base of under \$30k per year, which is basically minimum wage in California. Unless they’re getting serious bonuses, their employer is most likely breaking the law. Note \-- the survey started in August 2020 and a few agencies have materially increased their starting wages since.   
The [MIT living wage calculator](https://livingwage.mit.edu/) gives a figure of \$40k per year (single, no kids) to live in Los Angeles County; shame on the 15 employers in this survey reportedly paying less than this.  
Average rent in Los Angeles is \$2,000 to \$2,500 per month depending on your source. Housing is generally considered affordable if you spend less than 1/3rd of your income on it \-- [Zillow dreaming as Devon calls it.](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-182)  
So a base salary of \$72k to \$90k is needed \-- you pretty much have to be a higher level DoD or above to live in Los Angeles in an affordable manner. Assuming you start as an intern, that’s 3-5 promotions (or what, 6-8 years??) into a career\!   
(Devon: Also to note, most early career filmmakers make peanuts from their first show/movie, many go into debt to actually make them.)  
Not a lot to say about VPs on up. I hope the data below helps you negotiate more $$$ at your next review. Have a good base and fight for as much equity/points/credits as you can\!   
It was difficult to run analysis on bonuses, a lot of them were arbitrary or completely discretionary. Bonuses based on actual performance metrics, like selling a show or film, were few and far between. Hard to hit a target that’s not defined.  
Analysis was done on 84% of the total respondents (n=564); some of the data was too hard to clean up, had obvious errors/duplicates, or didn’t neatly fit in a box. Most respondents were likely on the creative side of the business.   
Survey responses were collected from August 2020 to September 2021\. Dollar amounts are in USD and we’re assuming the majority of respondents are in Los Angeles. I was not involved in the creation of the survey, nor collecting the responses (nor was Devon). Data is taken at face value.  
**What can you do with this information?**  
Talk about it, share it\!  
The big takeaway from this data is how it quantifies affordability (housing / cost of living) in our industry. To zoom out and look at human history, high income inequality is the norm. However we are still living in a post-war [1950s economic/cultural legacy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nUFZ1_fC3Kw) of low income inequality (*for some*). It’s not your fault you’re probably getting squeezed. Economic gaslighting?  
**To the bosses** \- If you’re looking to make world class entertainment then you need a world class team. Treat your people as such, not just by throwing a pizza party twice a year but by making sure their pay *and hours* are such that they can thrive in an affordable way. [This guy](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/dan-price-ceo-on-why-minimum-salary-70000-works/) raised the minimum wage at his company to \$70k and business is booming. Invest in your people, it’s not just the humane thing but also the [profitable](https://www.wsj.com/articles/the-case-for-higher-wages-in-hard-times-11611241084) thing to do.   
A \$10m pay cut from any of these [high earning CEOs](https://variety.com/2021/biz/news/hollywood-executives-disney-netflix-fox-1234962314/) can mean a \$5k raise to 2,000 employees; on the lower levels that’s game changing\! Call it late-stage philanthropic capitalism? It'd surely bring good publicity and improve employee morale. Positivity is profitability.  
The tides of human history are against you, and yes it might have been tough when you were coming up the ranks, but ask yourself this \-- Is your legacy one of building a better world or perpetuating the problems of the past? Just because you had to crawl through the mud, don’t make the next person.   
I moved to Hollywood with more savings than most and strong family support. My past work experience in finance didn’t mean much and I had to start all over again (unpaid internship). Yet, after my first 3 years I was mostly tapped out, skipping meals (pride stopped me calling home for help) and very unsure if I was on a sustainable path.   
I say all this to highlight that if I found it hard, I fully believe how it can be near impossible for many others. Diversity isn’t just about making the hire, it’s about ensuring everyone can have a sustainable career.   
**To the employees** \- The single most powerful thing you can do is talk about your pay and conditions. So thank you for those that did. There are strong federal laws that protect this, [look them up\!](https://www.govdocs.com/can-employees-discuss-pay-salaries/) I’m staggered by how many people don’t know this. A rising tide really does lift all boats, read more on this [here](https://www.realsimple.com/work-life/money/money-etiquette/how-to-talk-salary-with-coworkers), [here](https://www.washingtonpost.com/outlook/2020/09/06/millennial-salary-taboo/), [here](https://iwpr.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Pay-Secrecy-Policy-Brief-v4.pdf) and [here](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kimelsesser/2018/09/05/pay-transparency-is-the-solution-to-the-pay-gap-heres-one-companys-success-story/?sh=f6e3eda50100).   
If you find out a colleague is getting more, it’s not their fault. Direct those energies to management and see if your colleague can offer their support. You are not defined by your income, though it can feel like that.  
America is so divided on almost every issue; if there was a vote between free ice cream and a poke in the eye with a sharp stick it’d still be 51/49. That being said, I really hope that paying more, especially for more junior employees, is something that the industry can rally around. It brings more and diverse voices into the room, it’s profitable for companies, and most importantly it ensures the creative future of our industry.   
Let’s all see if we can nudge the needle towards a more righteous future.   
Cheers,
