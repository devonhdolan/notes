# Issue 71

Hello\! I’m in the South Island of New Zealand and in a constant state of overwhelm — this place is too beautiful. Jurassic. I’m happy I’m doing this now rather than when I have gray hair. Most of the people I’m meeting on the road are honeymooners or retirees. I feel very lucky. Ok, the Gimlet \+ Spotify announcement has been ripping through me. It actually kept me up for a couple of hours. I was questioning everything, even my own existence. So in the middle of the night, I opened up my notes app and starting writing about it, like a loser. \*Puts on sweater vest and AirPods\* I know enough about the content business, and corporate valuation, to be considered mildly dangerous (nowhere near lethal). On the heat scale, I’m a Sriracha aioli compared to some of you who are straight up ghost pepper extract. This \$200m+ price tag seems outrageous. Cash. I can’t come to a justifiable conclusion. Jaw, meet floor. Without the pertinent data, my continuous chin scratching won’t do many any favors. I shouldn’t even be dwelling on this event right now. When I see M\&A activity around content companies, I tend to toggle with the notion of buy vs build. Building a podcast studio isn’t that costly of an endeavor, especially for Spotify which has the infrastructure and audience. How attractive is their content? Gimlet has been around for about four years, and their small catalogue of IP is pretty solid — they’ve even done some film/TV adaptations and have more in the pipeline (thanks to CAA) — but I don’t see franchisable storylines. Narrative content inherently doesn’t scale unless there’s a robust universe of engaging characters (Harry Potter, Star Wars, Marvel, etc). Repeatability is a major issue for podcasts. I personally can’t listen to podcasts while working, perhaps that’s to my own detriment. Gimlet-produced podcasts are distributed on Spotify, Apple, and elsewhere, so maybe Spotify noticed that their subs were favoring Gimlet content over other forms of audio. That “minutes-listened” metric. I don’t know. Name-brand recognition isn’t it. The talent maybe, but this is far more than an acquihire. The video argument doesn’t move the needle at all, Spotify has tried their hand at it a handful of times. They’ve failed. Gimlet isn’t a video company, period. Tech companies are competing for our leisure time, so this might just be a move for in-transit entertainment. Gimlet could integrate well with their upcoming hardware and virtual assistant. Again, I don’t know. I’m just a bit baffled. I’m stoked for my friends who are Gimlet shareholders. Enjoy all of it. Jealous. Shoot me a message if you have eye opening opinions on the exit. Eager to tune in to Spotify’s earnings report this week. Any who, I’m waiting to see when a major tech co acquires a video game co (Nintendo, Epic, Activision Blizzard, EA, Ubisoft) — now that will make a lot more sense to me. A few of you have asked, I found Bandersnatch underwhelming. Too meta for my liking and I felt mislead, or had, one too many times. Amazon pretty much just bought Sundance. The times, how they are changing. That’s all for now. See you next week.
