# Issue 70

Hello\! I spent the weekend on Waiheke Island for a wedding, by far one of the most beautiful places I’ve ever visited. A couple of lengthy thoughts about topics in tech and media — this is a follow up to my 2019 predictions. 1\) I don’t know when the OTT craze will end (anotha one\!), but I’ve always felt that the major studios should pact, form a multi-party JV, and launch their own SVOD service. They could easily charge a pretty penny — dozens of dollars — and verifiably challenge the tech titans of Netflix, Apple, and Amazon. Hulu\* was a “botched” effort, and that’d make for a great B-school case study. Quibi has quite the investor list, and we’ll have to sit back to see how that pans out. The inside scoop is that many producers are repackaging their “mediocre” IP stuck in development hell for Quibi, and they’re lapping it up. Thirsty buyers. No bueno. The Meg hire still annoys me, deeply. This JV concept is what I haphazardly proposed about two years ago when the state of VR/360 distribution was in flux ([TC](https://techcrunch.com/2016/10/14/the-content-distribution-industrial-complex-for-vr/)). It’s not a novel idea, just VEVO rehashed. Re-bundled. I was inspired reading the OVID announcement last week, but let’s be real, no one really wants to watch whatever’s going to be on it. Total aside, I appreciate the Amazon Video Direct initiative for festival films where they pay producers a royalty based on per hour watched. Now that feels a bit more like a media meritocracy, coupled with a popularity contest. We jumped PVOD for LTV (lifetime value). It’d be nice if the legacy media companies were to actually play a positive sum game, with the audience in mind, because that’s ultimately who they’re serving. I believe that we’ll see piracy increase in 2020 because each studio is launching their own individual service for the benefit of themselves (and shareholders), and embargoing content across the web. It’ll end up being more expensive than cable subscribing to all. Ultimately, I think a Disney SVOD package, with a suite of content options, will win (shareholder disclaimer). 2\) On the note of piracy, I totally understand why people steal content and think VPN usage will increase. The media industry territorialized the world and artificially geo-blocks content based on air dates, holdbacks, and outputs. Even free ad-supported clips on YouTube. Do that and there’ll be negative repercussions. Provide people what they want, when and where they want it (worldwide). I can’t even watch last week’s SNL clips here in New Zealand, give me a break. 3\) Studios and networks need to experiment with new distribution concepts. HBO should book theaters for GoT on Sunday nights. Mirror Netflix’s feature strategy to an extent. I love that Netflix and HBO provide some of their shows for free on YouTube (Patriot Act, Last Week Tonight), and other networks upload their pilots on YouTube as well (Showtime’s Kidding). I’m sure all we’ll see though are exclusive windows. 4\) Micro influencers that have grown organically, including virtual ones, will rise in demand because their relationship with the audience is palpable and the numbers are believable. No botting. Verify the conversion metrics. Last week’s issue featured a good article penned by Jon Shieber about Brud and its competitors ([TC](https://techcrunch.com/2019/01/14/more-investors-are-betting-on-virtual-influencers-like-lil-miquela/)). I firmly believe that virtual influencers are only as talented as their creators, and the accounts managed by non-creatives will fall flat and stagnate (best wishes trying to develop creativity, then outsourcing it). Trevor of Brud is talented AF so that statement doesn’t apply to that company. A handful of these virtual influencers are nearly monetizable in all the same ways as real artists (filmmakers, musicians, designers). It goes without saying that creating fame on social and being actually talented IRL are totally different. Being highly followed does not mean one’s an artist (duh). I anticipate a Lil Miquela reality show on a digital platform in the next year or two, an album, a fashion line is a given, and eventually a live tour (holograms). Copy the Kardashians, but be authentic (and better). Mascot marketing with a message. Yay alliteration. 5\) Blockchain. I’m watching it closely now more than ever, and am researching the foundation-level companies involving network client relations around IP addresses, anon or not. Infrastructure — the ground floor. The internet needs to be re-written to an extent. Actually decentralized. Those building blockchain companies now have undying conviction. I might even start nibbling at crypto again. Probably not. Remember when crypto was called a hedge against the economy? Humor. If you want to make a buck building a blockchain company in Hollywood, lay tracks (smart contracts) between the guilds and distributors/producers, in the USD (no weird coin). That removes a giant pain point for everyone. I find that idea too boring to pursue full-time, but let me know if you’re into it and I’ll help. That’s all for now. Happy Sundance. See you next week.
