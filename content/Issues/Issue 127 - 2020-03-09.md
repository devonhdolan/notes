# Issue 127

Hello\! I’ll start by saying let’s all try to be reasonable and practical in this strange time. I’m travelling to Lisbon this week, and will be in London the beginning of next. Airports are looking like barren wastelands. I’m trying not to let the pandemonium get me down. It’s very easy to succumb to it. Don’t panic. Be cool. I don’t think it helps matters that I’ve watched movies like Contagion, World War Z, I Am Legend, and 28 Days Later the last few weeks. Contagion feels pretty bang on. Verge did a study with Torrent Freak on the [uptick in its popularity the last two months](https://www.theverge.com/2020/3/7/21164769/contagion-streaming-netflix-hulu-cinemax-itunes-download-torrent-coronavirus). Wild.  
I recently stumbled upon this collective of educational content creators called [Standard](https://standard.tv/pages/about), who banded together to form their own SVOD called Nebula with support from Curiosity Stream. Many gained their initial audience over YouTube and have since branched out to podcasts, blogs, and more. I follow some of the movie analysts and theorists like Lessons from the Screenplay, Patrick Willems, and science channels like Minute Physics. They’re throwing videos behind an exclusive paywall. It’s like a modernized Vessel. Curious to see how it unfolds. Cheering for them.  
A friend and I were chatting last week about how Netflix and other SVODs have the richest metadata on celebrity faces through their A/B tests of pictures as tile art. They might fundamentally change the presentation of movie posters, and album and book covers. Their title artwork is all about which key piece of cast is most clickable for whoever’s watching. They take into account your location and which genre you most often indulge in. It makes so much sense that they signed up Sandler to another massive deal. The audience generally knows what to expect when they click his face. I wouldn’t be surprised if they start doing the same with more actors who generally traffic in a specific genre to create a steadier pipeline of IP. Then, in-house creative executives will largely just service these talent deals. A glorified account manager of sorts. I don’t believe it’ll fully play out like this, but it is already happening.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
