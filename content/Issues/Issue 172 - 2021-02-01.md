# Issue 172

Hello\! So it’s Sundance. A now virtual place for celebration, though I’ve only really known it as a market (boohoo). It’s an onslaught of reviews and deals if you care to follow along. There’s been a big splash. Coda sold for \$25m to Apple. Huge. A new record, but to be fair, I think a new record has been set every year the last five years.  
I hear a lot of people cry when watching it, which means it may (or may not) appeal to the grey-haired academy. Probably yes as I’d wager the iPhone-maker dropping 25 mil (a rounding error for them) at the festival is a play for an Oscar. I’m slightly surprised they haven’t yet acquired a studio and its library. They have almost \$200b cash (\!) on hand. But a studio alone doesn’t bring home the trophy, that oh so elusive Oscar, which is nearly unquantifiable by a tech conglomerate… by anybody.  
And on a supremely morbid and irrelevant note, I recently learned that some businesses and insurance shops have quantified the value of a human life: \$200k on average. It goes back to the [Ford Pinto days in 1970](https://philosophia.uncg.edu/phi361-matteson/module-1-why-does-business-need-ethics/case-the-ford-pinto/) \- and the value is now, uhm, [roughly the same at \$200k](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/05/11/upshot/virus-price-human-life.html)… though case dependent. If you were to account for inflation from the 70s, it should be \$1.3m. This is taboo. Pandemic. Gross. Moving on.  
Fun fact: my old bossman John Sloss, [who needs to update his wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sloss), made headlines and set some records at Sundance as a sales agent with Little Miss Sunshine, \$10.5m to Fox Searchlight in 2006 ([IndieWire](https://www.indiewire.com/2016/01/10-huge-sundance-film-festival-deals-and-how-they-paid-off-30518/)), and more recently with the doc Knocking Down the House, \$10m to Netflix in 2019 ([IndieWire](https://deadline.com/2019/02/knock-down-the-house-10-million-dollar-netflix-deal-alexandra-ocasio-cortez-sundance-festival-documentary-renaissance-1202550755/)).  
I’m pretty familiar with how the numbers work having studied thousands of film income statements and played pretend as an excel jockey with film ultimate models. Maybe one day I’ll shed some light on the practice. It’s purposefully shrouded in mystery. But back to it.  
The numbers. The producer’s collection account, the waterfall, is a pittance really. The industry is horrid. There are so many hands and pockets with those low eight figure sales and the majority of indie films go for much, *much* less \- in the mid six fig range. And there’s not much hope thereafter nowadays. Excluding 2020 (I pray Minari will kill when it opens in two weeks), the last few US Grand Jury Prize winners did less than \$1m at the *domestic* box office: 2018’s Miseducation of Cameron Post (\$905k) and 2019’s Clemency (\$365k). If you’re lucky and strike gold in Park City, you might be able to afford a down payment on a home in LA. But you’ll have “made it” and springboard to the greener pastures of tinseltown where your fees increase. Cultural clout, buttered.  
The indie film microcosm exists as a labor of love. It is ridiculously hard. So few are in it for the money, except maybe a handful of representatives. But to be frank, you can’t be. You won’t last. On a dollar by hour basis, you’re probably better off doing anything else. Pay complete disregard to the cost benefit analysis. I’m talking more for the people on the creative side, who eat what they kill. It moves at a glacial pace. It’s a constant stream of No’s. You gotta be tough as nails. All my respect to those who are able to trudge onward, chin up. Hell, I still feel like I’m on the outskirts trying to fight my way into it, purely as an independent. It is totally, undeniably **absurd**.  
But as Camus said, “The struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a man’s heart. One must imagine Sisyphus happy.”  
I’m going to make this a *sort of* two-parter as I need to get to emails and immigration paperwork. Hats off to all you independent storytellers out there. Much love.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Work for the project. See you next week.
