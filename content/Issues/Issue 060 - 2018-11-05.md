# Issue 60

​​Hello\! This is a bit premature because my thoughts aren't fully-formed, but I've been thinking about the different use cases, workflows, supply chains, and business models for 3D assets and graphics. My first angle of attack is to begin with 3D's media and entertainment applications because that's the base layer from which I view the world, and well, filmmakers pioneered this field. A couple of fun facts. All blockbusters (3D animated or live-action) nowadays rely heavily on VFX and CGI. The first 3D computer animated film was Pixar's Toy Story (1995). The last released 2D animated film was Winnie the Pooh (2011), though many mobile games and television shows are still 2D. I'm not going to get into 3D film exhibition because I don't think it fits here, and I don't necessarily like it \- nor do I know many people who do\*. Plus, I think it was, and still is, a cash grab by the studios. It worked for Avatar \- perfect timing. (\*People don't like wearing glasses or HMDs on their face, which is probably why VR for entertainment didn't take off. But it was a great entree for me to get into these topics so I'm thankful for it.) To begin, I'm interested in new ways of creating (generating, scanning) and distributing (monetizing, visualizing) 3D digital objects. From AI in photogrammetry to LiDAR point clouds, 3D is going to become its own industry. I think that new approaches to ML/AI, with 3D riding passenger, will cause the next technological revolution, and 5G will catalyze it for mobile. 3D is the foundation for any immersive project. Augmented reality and light-field displays absolutely necessitate 3D models. In short, 3D is about adding a complex plane (Z and/or S) to give depth to an object, which creates volume. (A couple of you are wizards in the maths and physics of 3D, so please correct me if I'm astray.) There's already a whole suite of applications to serve this industry: real-time rendering engines, powerful GPUs and AI chips, and cloud-computing systems for big data because 3D file sizes are massive. Major technology companies will serve this as a full-stack portfolio, like Nvidia, Otoy, Unity, and Sketchfab. Other sub-sectors of 3D include aspects of motion capture and computer vision (image \[facial\] recognition). There's a lot of white space to introduce new ideas and methods as well. On a final note, outside of the film/TV bubble I live in, I think the construction and real estate sectors will be significantly impacted by these developments. AutoCAD and 3D printed homes/buildings seem to go hand-in-hand to me. That's all for now... I wish I was way more technical in this area. See you next week\!
