# Issue 10

Content creation from legacy media formats to new ones has been, and remains to be, a devolutionary act. The idea of a filmmaker or a showrunner embracing and iterating upon modern, shorter forms of content has yet to take in the market. I’m unsure why that is, but think it’s due to the notion that a creator’s career must progress toward the ultimate goal of film/TV — the ‘pinnacle’ of success. There are few creators/producers that seem keen on being able to make all forms of content, but in my mind, that’s the future. The one’s who do, such as Reese Witherspoon and Neil Blomkamp, will be able to speak multiple industry languages and tap into their respective markets. One thing’s for certain, the representatives and executives behind creators must inform themselves of the business models and economics of the broader ‘temee’ landscape to position the best transmedia storytellers to succeed. Pigeonholing oneself in a niche sub-sector will not lead to broad advancement, though working within a genre across media channels will cement one’s notoriety in that particular field, like Jason Blum (horror) and Lorne Michaels (comedy). All I’m trying to say is that a storyteller should not limit themselves to a particular form of creation, and the people backing them must be versed in all the different modes of creation and distribution in today’s ecosystem.
