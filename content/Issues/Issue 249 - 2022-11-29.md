# Issue 249

Hello\! This is my second to last issue in this form. I’ll be going dormant for a while. I’ll return with sporadic announcements and semi-regular essays/op-eds that can serve more purpose and act as standalone pieces. No more link lists, sorry.  
Placation. Vacation.  
Pride ultimately loses, or refuses to continue. I should’ve done this “overhaul” years ago, but my ego stood in my way. It often does. The purist’s scoundrel, a devil of my own making.  
Style, shaped… sculpted.  
This bloggy experiment always had no titles, no real marketing, no guest posts (well, two), no images, no corpo support, no easy interviews nor podcasts, no current event talk. Just raw text straight from the heart. My game, my rules. Hot takes let fly. I thought I enjoyed it that way.  
Until inadequacy washed over me. I might’ve cared too much. I liked that these emails reminded people I exist… that gave me some semblance of satisfaction – open rates are a leg up on likes too. Brain over brawn. I toggled with monetization – it’s been off the last six months. I made enough to buy a box of Franzia. It gave me some structure and routine \-- procrastinate on links until Sunday night and write the opening in a fit of fury on Monday morning.  
An outlet. I wanted it to feel original, to hone and own my voice. This was a manual process: hand-curated, hand-written. Hand-wrung. At least it gave me something to point to…as so much of my work disappears on calls and in emails. One recipient, lest forget. Lost to the interwebs.  
Never give up. Never surrender. But know when to quit, on a high note. Cut your losses.  
If I were to throw out wild guess numbers, I say averaged about a 500-word count in each preamble. Multiplied by 250, that’s around 125k words. Average novels are about 90k words, a couple hundred pages. That’s a lot of word spew.  
Cost benefit analysis. The rewards were intangible, but they were there, I convinced myself of it. Revenue introduces expectations and places even higher standards on the self. The product must be sustainable.. a growth engine this was not – and that was by my (foolish) design. I hold myself accountable. I think I did alright in the end. Washed up and dried out. What’s not to risk.  
Narrative excess. I find most writing to be absent of life. It’s euthanized. Dull and drab, the same shit on repeat. Never succumb to it. Allow a morsel of your personality to protrude. You owe it to yourself, it’s all you’ve got. There are no guilty pleasures.  
Owe no man anything, save love. \-some Bible quote.  
I have a lot planned for 2023\. I’m going to be a dad, to a girl. The greatest gift. We’ll likely move back to the states next summer. The NZ air is too clean for an infant, it should develop immunity rolling around the filthy streets of NYC. I’m starting a new company very soon. I tend to time my raises perfectly with market corrections and world catastrophes, but this time there won’t be stagnation. That is death. The tides are turning in my favor, momentum is swelling. I’m not letting go. It is oxygen. There’s already interest.  
If you’d like to know more. Just reach out. I promise only good things might come of it. Door is always open, for anyone. Do it. Send an email to dhd@wavemagnetventures.com  
The gist. It’s a storyworld accelerator and creator collectives protocol. A story as a startup venture studio. If it sticks, it’ll change up the industry a bit. I’m taking a page out of the Japanese system and YC. I feel really good about it. It’s an amalgam of all my ideas. It’s a system, a game, of people agreeing to play on similar terms. It’ll attract who I want it to – storytellers who think of themselves as startup founders. There’ll be capital constraints and deadlines. Scrappy cooperation. Earners. Most systems are full of fat. The body craves lean meat.  
Flipped and grilled. Clean slates, stack plates.  
The way I think of it, the creative industry is business (writer) to business (producer) to business (financier, and/or studio or streamer or publisher), then to consumer (theatrical rentals/tvod) to business (pay 1 deals). The relationships might look something like b(2b(2b(2c2b))). Please don’t look at that as a formula… more so transactional logic gates, where control and ownership are lost in exchange for peanuts. In perpetuity. Pemdas. Extraction increases as we flow to the right, most of the monetary windfall occurs in the first four months of release.  
Backend buyouts are a plague.  
Artist to audience. Relationships. This is still a sketch, but I’m thinking more like (b2b)(2b2c). I might have to study up on binomials again for all you algebra geeks, the FOIL method. Leverage the platforms, their audiences and checkbooks, with short-term licenses to a singular piece of expand-able media. Go direct, always. Easier said than done.  
Dialed, up. Quality is everything. The value chain needs to collapsed: increase the pace, increase the upside. All actions are boiled down to a 2x2 at the end of the day. Economic value and time. Short and long. Long-Long only  
Choppy waters subside. Set ships to sea. Anchors away.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Different strokes. See ya next week. 1 left.
