# Issue 157

Hello\! Day 9 here in New Zealand managed isolation. As of today, it’s a covid-free country (again). If you missed it, I wrote a bit about the journey in last week’s issue [here](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-156-9-28-20-858b4fa750a3?source=friends_link&sk=e7e8aee5350e20925f0dcd9ad9656075). This feels like the final leg of a prison sentence. I would know (jk). I had to change my location / Google settings as my search results weren’t tailored to my NYC/LA liking. I got bombarded with a bunch of kiwi ads, which was strange. Turned that off with a flip of the switch. And now I’ll need to use a VPN more often to watch shows like SNL on YouTube and elsewhere. Geo-blocked. Though Aus/NZ are generally where tech firms A/B test new products on users first, so that’s neat.  
I’ve been doing a lot of self-reflection in quarantine, and all of this year really. Namely pounding my head against the wall, and throwing shit against it too. I keep getting distracted from what I really, truly want to do. I turn 30 on Saturday.. that doesn’t help matters. Existential dread is real, on top of everything else that’s going on. My friend aptly called it the dopamine slide. I’ll touch on this next week \- the dynamics of social proof. Quarter-life crisis. Any who, I’ll be set free on Saturday and fly up to Auckland to spend precious time with the in-laws the next year+. That’ll be nice :)  
A longer, insider-y one today as I have a few thoughts and trends to note around Range Media Partners (fka Moxie Media \- I shared a super early version of their deck in [issue 152](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-152)), and very well-capitalized upstarts like CeleBrands ([Dead](https://deadline.com/2020/09/dick-clark-prods-allen-shapiro-john-howard-celebrands-ryan-reynolds-aviation-gin-kardashians-1234574411/)). First, it’s too soon to tell what’ll happen with Range. Do I think they’ll be successful? Yes, certainly. Dismissing that team is foolhardy. Within a month their “client” list crushes many of their competitor’s, though I’m hearing a lot of these are non-exclusive. Polyamorous talent. The nature of the business is built on relationship banking, plain and simple. It is heartily transactional. Help me, help you. Jerry MacGuire.  
Agents are jumping ship to become managers/producers, this really isn’t surprising at all. It’s been happening the better part of the last decade (if not longer). Agents have been internally producing for every medium under the guise of the packaging department. Take the fee, not the credit, and skim off the top while you’re at it. I was (still am) intimately involved in this racket. Bring the talent, money, and/or distribution. Talent above all else wins. If everybody’s a producer, then no one is. Only a handful really mean anything anymore. Most are deadweight. \*cries in the mirror\*  
It looks like company building is finally en vogue in Hollywood beyond just vanity production shingles, the majority of which are just loan-out corps with a cute first-look. Believe it or not, many stars don’t actually like doing the work. You know the ones who do because they’re everywhere: Kevin Hart, Ryan Reynolds, Rihanna, LeBron, Will Smith, Reese, Oprah, Kardashians, Kanye etc. When I was just starting out, I remember colleagues poo-poo’ing Alba for starting Honest Company and others questioned Kutcher’s venture motives. Who’s laughing now?  
As I flipped over the Moxie deck, I was reminded of the Entourage scene where [Vincent Chase meets with agents](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F613dbnA_WE) and they all pitch him on becoming a brand entity. Merch is the word, at first at least. I think the most valuable thing Range could do is start (or have sugar daddy Steve Cohen buy) a white-label product (apparel) manufacturer and streamline the supply chain (DTC) so their clients can quickly offer bespoke tee-shirts, hand towels, whatever. Drop ship with an integrated payment processing provider (Stripe, Shopify, Fast), and handle all operational logistics (SKUs etc) and legal / tax infra. Offer a healthy syndicate pool to all their clients. Account manage. This is foundational & full-stack. Templatize consumer goods by way of pop-up online storefronts. Throw in an ESG component for good measure, make it a Benefit Corp. Copy the sock company Stance to an extent (margins). Paul Newman is the OG. All easier said than done though \- like did you know McConaughey had a clothing co called [Just Keep Livin](https://justkeeplivin.com/)’. Or do the same with a luxury credit card and get the mega-rich hooked on it as a status symbol \- MasterCard did [just acquire \*to shutter](https://techcrunch.com/2020/08/24/mastercard-acquired-and-shut-down-ifonly-an-experiences-marketplace-hit-by-covid-19/)\* IfOnly. Where do I send my invoice?  
Conversion above all else. Financial is what matters most, not trading in audience follower-ships \- those are for your own ads. Consumer industries are tough, no doubt, but it’s easier to get some points on the board. Tech-first businesses are way harder, though hyper scale and escape velocity is what’s most attractive about ‘em. Starting any tiffany-level (lol) brand is Sisyphean. (Quick aside, I wouldn’t be surprised if Ovitz or Climan initially coined the tiffany phrase as I’ve heard a few CAA diehards say it.) And reading about CeleBrands reminded me of Star Avenue Capital, when CAA was in cahoots with PE firm Irving Place back in 2010 ([BW](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20100513006278/en/Former-Head-of-Bear-Growth-Capital-Partners-Paul-Lattanzio-to-Lead-Star-Avenue-Capital)) to buy consumer businesses like J Brand. That same year they helped launch WhoSay, which Viacom acquired in 2018 ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WhoSay)). Speaking of CAA, a year later they formed a sort of incubator with Qualcomm [called Mobile Labs in 2011](https://variety.com/2011/digital/news/caa-marries-talent-apps-1118031632/) to start apps with their clients (aka Moonshark). Together, they made the Tom Hanks typewriter app with developer Hitcents, who eventually bought Moonshark in 2014\. And in 2017, CAA would start the exact same incubator model but call it Creative Labs with backing from eOne and Boat Rocker ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/10/27/startup-studios-go-hollywood-as-caa-launches-creative-labs/)). And this summer they announced an early stage fund with NEA called Connect. Then there’s the whole other beast that is WME, like, remember theAudience ([TC](https://techcrunch.com/2012/07/26/theaudience/amp/)) I really wish I could delete this shit from my brain…  
I don’t think many industry stakeholders understand the new world of media and how social is the primary marketing (sales) channel \- which is why marketing depts at studios have so much power. Like this [new music mgmt firm Mint](https://variety.com/2020/music/news/mint-talent-group-agency-wme-paradigm-caa-1234778378/) says they offer clients a 47-point marketing plan. What the hell does that mean? Over-complicated. This conversation recently infiltrated tech/vc twitter around influencers. Here’s a [Taylor Lorenz quip](https://twitter.com/TaylorLorenz/status/1312487573084807169) that caused a silly kerfuffle. I think influencers have little staying power though \- I say this as someone who helped orchestrate a deal with a touring influencer con festival thing (gross, I know) in 2014, and worked with Influential four years before WME partnered with them ([Var](https://variety.com/2018/digital/news/wme-influential-investment-social-data-platform-talent-branded-content-1202824972/)). Kevin Kelly of Wired fame [wrote the 1,000 true fan article](https://kk.org/thetechnium/1000-true-fans/) in 2008\. Embrace the principals. Ok, I’m done with this rant.  
Final observation, I’m seeing Warner Bros drop opening scenes on YouTube, such as [V for Vendetta](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1nRfc2pEaCc). A fishhook to HBO I presume. This is going to become more of the norm. Your opening better be undeniable \- I put down one too many scripts/books after reading the first 10 pages \- and I think these openers will soon be made on the cheap by scrappy filmmakers to set the stage for their feature for executives to be able to envision it. A pilot, if you will, that captures the aesthetic. Better than a ripomatic. Think Showtime’s Kidding was one of the first do this, which I noted in issue 51 two years ago. Alas, audiences are being trained to expect more free content. Not good.  
That’s all for now. Sorry for the painfully long email today. Thanks for reading it. See you next week.
