# Issue 41

First and foremost. There is a humanitarian crisis at the Mexican-American border. Please take action by either donating your time, voice, and/or money to the cause. I said it in issue 24, and I will say it again. I’m with the kids. I’m so fed up with this administration. To those fighting the good fight (investigative journalists and human rights lawyers) keep up the good work. Nothing else really matters right now.  
Second. I hope you all capitalized on the release of IGTV by attaining some of that sweet, sweet social currency. I find it intriguing when I test out new apps/products and see people trying to become power users. It’s quite the phenomena. Early videos on IGTV got millions of views… I wonder if it could be considered some form of insider trading \[to increase your social worth/value\] if you were in the know and had great content at the ready?\! Sadly, I don’t think the in-app experience of IGTV will be a success, especially with Stories an easy click away. I don’t care about lengthier edited videos (some polished, many not) if I can get distracted with a friend’s story. That’s my guilty hit of serotonin. Users will remain idle — creatures of habit — though I’m sure FB will come up with elaborate spin for shareholders and advertisers alike. This’ll be a wild attempt to alter consumer behaviors, and we’ll see a lot of (paid?) influencers directing their followers to the new tab — certainly when videos are monetized. Enjoy this new form of self-promotion.  
Third. I re-listened to the Moby x Bret Easton Ellis podcast this week ([link](https://podcastone.com/episode/B.E.E.---Moby---9/12/16-1674029)). These pull quotes from Moby really stuck with me. “When we were growing up, there was a trajectory. Odd idiosyncratic people could made something like music, art literature — a product of their oddness and idiosyncratic nature. And their creation propelled them to fame. The paradigm shift was when good-looking people became famous for being good-looking, and then they used their fame to make stuff.” “Culture, like music, art, literature, didn’t have as much competition as we were growing up. And now, a 15 year-old kid has so many profoundly compelling things in his life.” This got me thinking a bit about “le wrong generation.” Times are different nowadays, obviously. I like listening to BEE’s divisive rants. His arguments are well constructed and delivered, and he has little sympathy. I think he’s one of the most thoughtful pop culture critics in the world right now. Needless to say, Moby is pure genius. The top three albums that affected me the most when I was a kid were Play, Led Zep IV, and Sgt. Pepper’s Lonely Hearts Club Band (Californication is way up there too). Anyway, I’m at my boss’s upstate house for our company retreat today — white boards, swims suits, and tennis rackets are in tow. Happy Monday. See you next week.
