# Issue 59

Hello\! So it’s been a minute since I’ve gone a salty rant, and I feel the need to vent. Plus, I always get emails afterward so it makes this newsletter a bit more fun and conversational for me. Okay, I’m frustrated with people who shower others with praise for coming up with “new” content formats, when really all they’ve done is slightly alter a running time. Big deal. Give me a break — that isn’t innovative whatsoever. From a dude launching a 5-min podcast to Quibi’s whole value proposition — I find this massive buy-in premature and underwhelming. Having grown up with YouTube and Vimeo, I think the people recognize those places for being the home of \<10-min video content. Plus, it’s free (even though the majority is mind-numbing UGC). I was speaking with a colleague the other day and he mentioned that if Katzenberg’s theory doesn’t pan out, then Katz will just shift his strategy and create long-form content. Simple as that, another Netflix competitor. The thing is, Netflix already has the subs and capital to flip a switch and compete with Quibi. That’s a serious competitive advantage. Like any major incumbent, they can just go on a warpath to squash the upstart. Need a tangential example, just look at Snap and Instagram. IG Stories launched 2 years ago and it has absolutely poisoned Snap, who shed 2m DAUs last quarter. While we’re on the topic, I’m still trying to make sense of IGTV — please tell me if you actively watch videos there because everybody I’ve ever asked has replied in the negative. I think IG users generally hang out on the Discover page to be delivered quick bites (ugh) of entertainment. Alright, I’m done with these ramblings. The offensive and defensive corporate strategies around short-form will be fun to watch in the next two years. To win, the silver bullet will always be the content itself. That’s all for now. See you next week.
