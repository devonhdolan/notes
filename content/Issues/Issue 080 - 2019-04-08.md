# Issue 80

Hello\! I’m currently in Osaka. This city is wild with juxtapositions. Tranquil temples then street market mayhem. Sensory overload at both ends of the spectrum. I’ve had a couple of conversations now using Google Translate and emojis. It’s like Pictionary with real-life consequences. Good fun. I’ve been journaling every day, a great exercise, and intend to keep it up when I return stateside. It’s an easy way to revisit my destinations. Speaking of, a dozen people have requested my Japan recs, but I don’t really like making itineraries. The asks got me thinking about another zany idea. Prompt-based reviews. A better way to initiate engagement. The meat of the market in getting someone to transact is done by crafting a story to get the customer to the point of sale. A direct recommendation from a trusted source, or many sources, is usually the best marketing tactic. Word of mouth. Buy-in. Airbnb mastered strong-arming reviews and ratings from users. I think the companies that own payment systems (Apple, Square, Visa, Amex, crypto \[?\]) could send a notification after-purchase asking for a quick review, which could be done in text dialogue format where you can add a photo for extra points. An avatar or voice assistant could also facilitate this interaction. Then, this feedback could be synced back to your default map (Apple, Google) completed with a social layer (Snap, Foursquare’s Swarm). Users would be able to adjust their privacy settings for everyone, friends, personal, or anonymized. I could then easily share my activity log, my journal, with people who ask me for Japan recs. I think this could be a Black Mirror episode — the call and the response. I don’t know the laws governing and regulating these industries, just some food for fodder. Cash would become king again, and I would certainly manipulate my reviews as to not blow up my local digs. Tragedy of the commons. Open source your ideas. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
