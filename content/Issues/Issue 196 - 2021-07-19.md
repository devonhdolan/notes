# Issue 196

​​Hello\! Movies on the mind, always. I’ll explain below, but I’m starting a weekly movie club. [Fill out this super quick Typeform if you want in](https://devondolan1.typeform.com/to/sjE6qlFQ). Or reply direct with your name, preferred email, and a classic film you’re embarrassed to say you haven’t seen. You can text me if you’ve got my number. No judgement, low commitment \- just watch an indelible movie a week. Perhaps we’ll talk about it as a group, perhaps not. Everyone will be anonymous on bcc. It’ll be a safe space. That’s it. All for fun.  
So Tarantino is making the rounds promoting the novelization of Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, and whenever I listen to him I feel woefully inadequate. The guy is a movie encyclopedia. I am not.  
I’m embarrassed to say I haven’t seen most of the stuff he rattles off. A deep shame boils inside me. I’d rather not publicly admit it, but I’ll start with some examples. I watched Wong Kar-wai’s Chungking Express for the first time in an arthouse theater last week — the legendary filmmaker behind In the Mood for Love.  
I’ve never seen Hal Ashby’s Shampoo, nor Pedro Almodóvar’s Talk to Her, Melville’s Le Samouraï, Cronenberg’s Videodrome, del Toro’s Mimic. The list goes on.. please don’t yell at me. But yeah, these are the options for the first week. I’ll probably run a short poll in a separate email for those who opt-in to cast a vote, or at the top of these issues cluing you into the film of the week. Interactive and communal. Joyous.  
On the topic of Wong Kar-wai, if you live in Williamsburg/Bushwick, check out the bar Mood Ring. The place takes aesthetic inspiration from his films. The bar is into astrology if that’s your jam, a new religion for so many. It’s not for me, but everyone’s got a vice… I’ll stick with palm readings (I joke). My local watering holes were Easy Lover, Anchored Inn, Jupiter Disco, and you’d most often find me at Yours Sincerely. More recs below. I miss NYC terribly.  
Any who, Tarantinto is also starting a podcast with Roger Avary (Pulp Fiction co-writer) where they’ll discuss the days and films of Video Archives. I need to know what they’re talking about, so I promised myself I’d do a better job at divulging in the classics. At least one a week. If you want to join me, [please do](https://devondolan1.typeform.com/to/sjE6qlFQ). Erase your blind spots. Remove choice paralysis.  
So yeah you might need a Criterion or Mubi subscription. Some of these you might need to rent for \$4 a pop, god forbid. Others might be harder to find… a super early idea percolating here, email if you want to help me organize a “where to stream” database (plus int’l with VPNs) and/or acquire small film libraries ;)  
Generational. These are the films that influenced the people who are influencing culture now. They probably inspired your boss and the writer/director you’re ultimately working for. The watch party is ephemeral, but the movie should create a lasting imprint on the mind. A filmmaker’s filmmaker. Cinephile, iconoclasm.  
Somewhat related, a friend brought up the recognizability of pop songs along the generations, [here’s a great Pudding article](https://pudding.cool/2020/07/song-decay/) on it with some charts and data. Also, most data from new streamer analysis companies is generally BS. Fun facts.  
Create memories, inflection points. It was a delight seeing Sam Neill in the 1981 creature feature Possession ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Possession_\(1981_film\))) at Syndicated BK, my weekly movie den. A group of us film execs would also go to Videology and watched movies like A Fish Called Wanda and others that were cultural touchstones in the 70s/80s that we all happened to miss. Good times.  
There are a host of new venture-backed startups popping up in the space, most geared for books (Bookblub, Literati, Fable)… so maybe a small business will emerge here. I’d give all early (and consistent) members ownership if anything eventuates. Auto-drive. And a special shoutout to the good people over at [Le Cinema Club](https://www.lecinemaclub.com/about/), I’m definitely taking a page from their playbook. Huge fan of their mission.  
On top of of all this, the Cannes winners were announced. I’m so fired up for Julia Ducournau’s Titane. Watch her first feature Raw ([wiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_\(film\))) if you haven’t yet, proceed with caution if you’re squeamish.  
To cap this off, I watch movies to switch on, to get inspired. The artistry of it all. Most people don’t. That’s fine. They go to the telly to get pacified, to turn off after a long day of work and fall into an indulgent stupor of The Office or Parks and Rec. I’d rather not.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Stretch the shelf life. See you next week.
