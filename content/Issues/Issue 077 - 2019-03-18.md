# Issue 77

Hello\! First, let me say that koalas are the cutest things ever. They’re just as you imagine. Adorable. Okay, so I had to delete the beginning of this week’s preamble because it was a tirade against certain influencers. But we must realize that posting daily content that leads to a real audience is a slightly impressive chore, no matter how narcissistic the act might be. I revert to what Moby said about fame, which now follows good-looking people that results in some uninspired artistic creation, rather than vice versa ([issue 41](https://mailchi.mp/ddb2928c89fb/temeeco-issue-41)). So what’s next might read sarcastic, but some iteration of the idea could work as many would argue that humans are innately status seeking monkeys. Here it goes, I think many social media stars can be manufactured in influencer academies. An established central figure should form an on-brand accelerator to increase followership amongst the cohort, really acting as a conversion engine (or subscriber fairy), to teach and knight new influencer pupils. The academy would simply instate income share agreements, broker sponsored posts and in-person appearances, help with merch, and even pitch investors like Next 10 on demo day. The business, gamed. It’s just a reworked bootcamp. This would likely work only for a few specific categories of social content (e.g. health, beauty, stunts), but certainly people can be productized. Gaming and comedy are their own separate beasts as they require more talent (there, I said it). 1600 Vine, a notorious influencer apartment building in LA, is like a warped modern-day printing press for influencers ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/12/30/business/hollywood-apartment-social-media.html)). Jake Paul attempted something similar with his Team 10 incubator house, which ultimately failed because it lacked direction ([Polygon](https://www.polygon.com/2018/5/8/17331622/jake-paul-team-10-chance-sutton-logan-greg-paul-youtube)). I imagine Casey Neistat intends to make the smarter version with his 368 space, which he likens to Warhol’s Factory 2.0. Is it Epicurean to be thirsty for followers? That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
