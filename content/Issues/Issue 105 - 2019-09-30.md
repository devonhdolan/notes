# Issue 105

Hello\! I’m back from early retirement. It’s a relief knowing I’ll be in one place (NYC) for at least the next year. Feel free to hit me up if you’re here and want to grab a coffee or something. Stacking up the calendar now. Okay. This is abstract, but I’ve been dwelling too much on reputation, status, and/or clout. Brand. However you might cut it. In June, I made mention of this topic in [issue 90](https://mailchi.mp/1d5d2fb0d8a1/temeeco-issue-90) by stating you’re defined by where you work, though I principally wrote about artificial consciousness. To briefly elaborate, that is because the firm is staking their reputation on the individual to an extent. The value of the pedigreed association. This is institutionalized in society starting with school. This immeasurable dynamic is fairly one-sided when you’re out to to earn your stripes, then once you have a few, you begin to transcend and carve out your own namesake. This certainly applies to the apprenticeship clout-transfer model. I mean knowledge. I feel that in today’s time, when someone’s reputation precedes them, that just means they’re great at online self-promotion, especially on Twitter (more on that another day — I find it to be a tasteless bubble). Social signalers. Credit snatchers. Resume blasters. Spam @s. The thing is, these people are just crafting how they want others to perceive them on a wholly external basis. It’s not personal, more pseudo peer-driven. I’m bothered (maybe jealous?) by a couple people who have gone on to some online notoriety in my same playground, but in reality they’re more or less ostracized from it. The public vs private disconnect. That said, I’m starting to nuke and privatize a couple of my profiles because I don’t want strangers to build an image of me, or my “path”, when they don’t actually know me. Brand biases. Whatever. At the end of the day, I believe that strong, verifiable relationships (not connections or followers) are the most heralded form of status. I’m reminded of what Jimmy Iovine once said to Larry Jackson — you’re your own fiefdom. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
