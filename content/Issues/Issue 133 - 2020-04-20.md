# Issue 133

Hello\! So I imagine there are going to be some new clauses in film/tv contracts. The freak show that is Tiger King has been sitting in the Netflix (U.S.) throne for a month. All the parties involved in the creation of it should be rewarded. I’m thinking reps will demand a “top 10 list” bonus if they’re negotiating with Netflix, based on length of (weekly) time spent in the charts. (This top 10 list is cause for concern. I thought I had a pulse on the public’s tastes: Wrong.) Think it’s a pretty good idea, and I haven’t heard any mention of the sort. Reap what you sow. Talent loves to play for upside, and they’ll get more excited about their Netflix press tours. Regarding conversion on press, I don’t know why studios aren’t capitalizing on the discussion when they announce a shift in a film’s release schedule in the wake of the pandemic. Films that were supposed to hit theaters this weekend will be available to stream or download in two months. Wait, what? We’re excited about it, bring it forward. Common sense. And I love that studios are making a lot of their pilot content available for free now too. Good stuff.  
And speaking of upside, studios are offloading films to the streamers. A handful of creatives play for a film’s backend, net profits, and they’re effectively bought out in streamer deals. As the old expression goes: there is no net. Well, usually. A friend told me that agents and lawyers are fighting for their clients to see additional compensation in these “dump” deals. I think this’ll be an uphill battle. The studio took more risk, and they’re probably barely breaking even on them, if that. Sucks. One of the world’s richest producers, Jason Blum, famously makes low-budget horror flicks (e.g. Purge, Paranormal) to entice talent to work at scale (or a far lower rate than usual) for a big share of the net. He fired about 25% of his roughly 30 person staff the other week, all juniors. A cowardly, piggish move. I stand in solidarity with my comrades in arms, underpaid Hollywood kinship. Don’t send him projects. I won’t. Asshat.  
One thing I’ve noticed is that people are actively supporting their friends and locals in these tough times. Tuning into livestreams to juice numbers, sharing their posts and blogs to increase visibility, etc. A sign of encouragement. Let’s move the needle. That’s what life’s all about. I think it’d be neat if you could allow for a sort of preferred, exclusive guest book on your site. Not just a comment section, but a section of love. People with big followings can help boost awareness to lesser known sites (uhm, hi). Last week, I stumbled through a bunch of Brooklyn-based product designers that linked to their friend’s portfolios. Altruistic signals. It led to a nice rabbit hole. On this note, a few of my friends launched betas recently. Cody made [Surf City](https://surf.city/1/), a lean-back internet tv experience with hand-selected channels (to later be localized). Joel and Rosie shipped [Analogue](https://analogue.app/signup?code=devonhdolan), a social destination for people to share their curated media diets. Curation, a common thread. No algo. Check them out if you’re so inclined (:  
Following on last week’s issue, I will pay Masterclass to not advertise to me. Google their name a few times and it’s a never-ending onslaught of ads. My lord, please stop.  
And finally, we donated blood yesterday. Do what’s right for the community. The nurse told me that if you verifiably tested positive \- and made it through the other side \- then your plasma is carrying antibodies. Go donate\! It could save lives. Nothing’s more meaningful. And if you didn’t have the virus, you should donate anyways. Seriously.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
