# Issue 155

Hello\! So, more big news from me on the personal front that I’ll share next week. There’s still one more line of defense, so I don’t want to jinx it, but I’ll let you know once I make it through customs(\!). Professional news should be rearing its head soon, publicly. The ol’ rolling thunder publicity approach. Excited to share when ready.  
Keeping this one rather short as I’ve got a lot on my plate in preview of this new life event \- I’ve subtly hinted at it the last few months in here with “brb”. The topic of today is pretty much a layup \- and I’m sure I’ve written about it before \- but here it goes: discovery and search on OTT apps is absolutely garbage. This is the whole “it takes me 20 mins to find what I want to watch” water-cooler conversation. It ain’t fun.  
We’re all probably most familiar with our default setting, which is accessed through our set-top-device (dongle) or smart TV. That’s Amazon’s Fire TV for me, and I think they’re top notch even if they do drive me to paywalled content every so often \- and if they do, take out your damn wallet and pay \$3 to rent a movie, that’s the cost of a coffee. Market research is all across the board, but it looks like Roku commands the US market. They were a nice stock to own when they IPO’d two years back :)  
Anyway, Amazon uses [X-Ray](https://www.cnet.com/how-to/everything-you-need-to-know-about-amazon-x-ray-for-tv-shows-and-movies/), which is powered by IMDB (Amazon owns that too). They plug into that data to show you who’s in what scene, and also categorizes content by director and talent in the fields below. (Psst, [IMDB has a public API](https://developer.imdb.com/).) Netflix and others generally sort by genre, which only gets you so far. Netflix notoriously has [1000s of codes](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/news/the-netflix-id-bible-every-category-on-netflix/) to “tag” content that their data scientists use for god knows what. I think Criterion is a close second, especially with their fanciful guest curators. Nothing’s more special than the human touch. My pops only watches movies with a score of over a 7.0 IDMB rating \- he’s my read on the masses \- and it’s absolutely outlandish that you can’t sort by rating (critic or audience \- like [Rotten Tomatoes also has a public API](https://developer.fandango.com/)), by country, by award winner etc. Mubi tries to remove choice paralysis with rolling 30 day licenses, but not too many know about them as their stuff is pretty niche :(  
I’ve met a handful of entrepreneurs who are trying to implement a search layer or “re-bundle” on top all the existing OTT (streamer) apps. I don’t think this’ll work. They’re trying to scrape and update a bunch of info to then deep link to it for their users. Touch sell, and back catalogues and output deals are always changing hands (or now downright being embargoed). If the entertainment companies were to play nice, there’d be a giant Dewey Decimal System for all film/tv content. Won’t happen. Walled gardens.  
I don’t really have a solution here, but I think the “customers also watched” is a decent associative technique to help people discover content. This at least surfaces films with a similar mood, aesthetic, or feel \- generally. Hawk-eyed on this space….  
Random asides: I’ve been digging Jeremy O. Harris’ mixtapes with his insta posts ([IG](https://www.instagram.com/jeremyoharris/?hl=en)), and also Daniel Askill’s insta stories where he shares a photo plus one word on it, which retains my attention like no other ([IG](https://www.instagram.com/danielaskill/?hl=en)). Plenty of fun new ways to tell stories on social media nowadays. They’ll continue to reveal themselves. Like, Bret Easton Ellis reading his upcoming book chapter by chapter on his Patreon podcast\*, and the comment threads of his audience trying to figure out the mystery is pretty entertaining. Serialized community. Probably something to do here….  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
