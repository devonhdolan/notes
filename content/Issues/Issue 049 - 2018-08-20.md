# Issue 49

Hello\! Let’s quickly talk about billboards. There’s been a flurry of out-of-home (“OOH”) advertising deals over the past few years, most notably with Netflix buying LA-based Regency Outdoor for \$300m, and my basic belief is that the reasoning for this activity is rooted in presence. Digital native companies are eager to make their mark on the physical world, and billboards are the fastest and cheapest catalyst to tangibility. I’m quick to hate on commercial advertising because it’s interruptive (and usually awful), but I find billboards intriguing because they’re more purpose-built. Sure they can be terrible and intrusive in their own right, especially if they impede natural beauty on a scenic-drive, but they’re made to drive home a message in the most succinct possible manner through objective or subjective association. If they feature a celebrity, they oftentimes enhance that person’s reputation. If done well, I think they’re one of the best outlets for brand building. Two of my favorite companies in the space are Colossal and Seen. They’re both based in Brooklyn and hire local artists to paint their billboards, which are usually reflective of our times and culture. They make art, not ads. Murals that are shareable that can create earned media. (Fun fact: the founders of Colossal also founded Mass Appeal.) I’m excited to see how the future of OOH advertising grows as more billboards become digitized and integrated with QR/AR features, which will impact the bottom line leading to an online-offline funnel system. A convergence of old and new media sensibilities. That’s all for now. See you next week\!
