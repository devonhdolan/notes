# Issue 173

Hello\! I’ll say it. The Super Bowl might be the best con job, a deviant ploy, to get people to *actually seek out* commercials. A brilliant, longstanding act of subterfuge. And I think we’re going to see more events and celebs cleverly sneak in “white labeled” adverts into our daily feeds, our programming schedules, our… timelines.  
Soon, it will be par for the course \- at least I think so anyway. Product placement or an expensive cameo don’t cut it in today’s age \- the “content” needs to be homegrown, designed in a bottom-up approach with a singular voice who shares your “brand ethos” at the center. I believe these things are called advertorials. I first noticed them in the bougie mag Monocle many moons ago \- here’s a little [Digiday puff piece](https://digiday.com/media/monocle-goes-native/) on it from 2013\. Think they got in hot-water for misleading readers. Hmm.  
It’s blatantly obvious, but we’ve now got celebrities directly marketing their brands or investments over social media. Ryan Reynolds is the patron saint. And I love it. He puts his own spin on these ads and it works. His film/tv production company gave birth to a successful advertising agency, where he’s on the board of some of their clients ([Match](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/ryan-reynolds-wendi-murdoch-join-board-match-tinder-parent-company-1301373)). Win-win-win scenarios: Ryan & co, the brand, and the audience. Fully integrated. Stacked. Maximum effort.  
But back to it: The Super Bowl. \$5m for 30 seconds of airtime \- curious if the network factors in the earned media here \- is quite a price to pay. And the buzz will be gone in a week. People overlook that the \$5m fee *excludes* the grossly inflated commercial budgets. Some of these spots easily run over \$1m just to make. I absolutely love filmmakers, producers, storytellers, and craftspeople getting PAID.  
I’m hoping one day I’ll be able to convince ad agencies and brands themselves to re-direct some of that cash to make brand-financed feature films/shows… potential assets. I’m eagerly anticipating your call, but I won’t hold my breath. I’ve been harping this tune for a few years now…  
We’ll also see the uglier shadow of this identity-sponsored trend too. Here are recent Tweets from the rapper [Ja Rule](https://twitter.com/jarule/status/1354927412836970496) (remember Fyre Fest) and adult star [Mia Khalifa](https://twitter.com/miakhalifa/status/1358343732622315521) (Google at own risk) pumping ridiculous schemes like GameStop and DogeCoin. I guess you can call that “the top”. Ba dum tss. Elon Musk, the richest dude on Earth, is a master market manipulator here by Tweeting stock memes and playing bait and switch with the SEC. It’s unreal. A fish will be fried. Money is a psychology. Feeding frenzy.  
Speaking of GameStop and bubbles, is there a way to short all these adaptations about the event? Hollywood is excellent at giving awards to itself and chasing the red hot thing. It can very much be a navel-gazing industry where herd mentality prevails. Last time we saw a similar string of announcements of ‘remake’ stories was with Tiger King and the Thai soccer team trapped in a cave. We’re still waiting, or maybe has society moved on. Flash in a pan.  
Though crafty (read: dastardly) producers will no doubt find their way into these stories. From buying a key person’s life rights, to optioning articles, making a doco, a podcast, whatever they need to do to make the *first*, significant announcement that others reference. Mark Boal and Ben Mezrich shared last week’s bylines. The race, be it rather litigious, is on.  
When they zig, you zag. Follow the pack and you will (over)pay in a bull market. But there’s some proof in the pudding. There’s an event, people, or topics that the audience will potentially recognize. The learning curve has been gently flattened. I’m working on a few things where I’m more or less reverse engineering marketing stunts (that are too technical for their own good) to essentially prove IP. Hope it works out.  
Speaking of advertorials \- I shamelessly plug the startups I’m working with in this preamble whenever I get the chance. It has led to some wonderful partnerships, some of which have resulted in million dollar+ happenings. Pretty cool. To that end, you’ve prob seen [Hovercast](https://www.hovercast.com/) in here a few times, a virtual events platform with interactive features. Eli, the co-founder/ceo, [wrote another banger of an article](https://medium.com/hovercast/interactive-live-streaming-guide-1949a576be59) elaborating the 5 C’s of making interactive television (curation, competition, choice, crowdplay, co-creation) based on a recent series they produced in partnership with GBH where Arthur \- you know, the cute fuzzy bear \- made a special appearance. Peep it\!  
And last week I wrote about Sundance film deal records and my old work friends at Cinetic… happy to see they [set another doc record](https://variety.com/2021/film/festivals/ahmir-questlove-thompson-summer-of-soul-documentary-searchlight-pictures-hulu-sundance-1234901465/) with Questlove’s Summer of Soul to Searchlight/Hulu for \$12m. Pretty sure this was the same project we had been circling over two years ago when I was there. Long lead time. Love to see friends doing well.  
And I did mention today’s issue would be a sort of part-two [drafting off last week’s note](https://medium.com/devons-newsletter/issue-172-2-1-21-c6834850b3a4?source=friends_link&sk=4031a6c5f2c083f41b1b53bc469a3bd6). Well, some of the market dynamics that I was alluding to can play out at Sundance. And I think indie Sundance titles need to be marketed to their customer (acquisitions teams) in preview of the festival, beyond just the run of the mill festival campaign with some hype calls to tastemakers and critics. Okay, enough.  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Tragedy of the commons. See you next week.
