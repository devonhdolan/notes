# Issue 75

Hello\! Or should I say, g’day. I’m in Australia for the month, road tripping up the east coast from Melbs to the Goldie. I welcome any hot tips of must-dos along the way — you can reply direct to this email. The kids here prefer to skate and bike rather than scooter, which I appreciate. Not a fan of e-scoots, but speaking of electric vehicles, I’ve been noodling on the data that hyperlocal transport, commute, and delivery systems can provide to media buyers to inform their out-of-home advertising spend (billboards), and used by online-to-offline decision makers on where to build retail locations. I’m fascinated by world-mapping and can’t believe this didn’t dawn on me sooner. Check-in data is what made Foursquare profitable, and valuable to Snapchat (geo-filters), and I imagine it being a substantial revenue generator for Lime and Bird (and all the others) since it’s nearly frictionless for their riders to hop on and off to consume (say, to buy a green juice). Last thing, I’ve been spending a lot of time on planes watching movies (some airlines even provide VR headsets to first class customers\!), and know that in-flight entertainment is generally viewed as a marketing expense by the airlines so that they can offer a better experience, and provide satisfaction, for their customers thus creating post-transaction loyalty. It’s not their primary business, it’s barely a business for them at all. (They do sell ads against programming, so it’s subsidized.) It’s disheartening to think that’s likely how many of the major tech firms will ostensibly view their entertainment initiatives. It’s a vanity, side project for them. A fun experiment. A rounding error on their financials. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
