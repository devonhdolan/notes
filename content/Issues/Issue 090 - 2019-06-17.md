# Issue 90

Hello\! I’ve been thinking a lot about sapience and sentience, nature versus nurture, and generally, the human condition. Psychology. I’m inspired by movies like Chappie, Transcendence, Ex Machina, The Matrix (my fave), and most recently, the Black Mirror\* episode with Miley. I’m thinking of the systems we use to define and label people. It sounds gross, like profiling or stereotyping. Engineers are working to create [artificial consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_consciousness) (AC) and I find it one of the most interesting areas of invention. At its core exists the question, how do we measure and qualify our attributes? Our cognition and character. It’s easy to point to the low hanging fruit of personality and IQ tests, but I don’t think they’re especially reliable as we’re in a constant state of evolution, adapting to our environment. Myers Briggs, and I guess some folks rely on their Zodiac (just, no), are simply examples of human matrices. Classifications. Side bar, it seems to me that people tend to define themselves by where they work rather than what they do. That’s unfortunate. Do we really place more emphasis on an employer’s brand equity rather than our own ambitions and accomplishments? We’re credential-seeking missiles, or as Eugene Wei puts it, [status-seeking monkeys](https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2019/2/19/status-as-a-service). Back to AC. When born, we’re defined by our demographics, and we eventually amass interests and behaviors as we’re shaped by our surroundings, which leads to psychographics. Bottom-up design through procedural programming. Choices. You know, life. ([Here](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/what-is-psychographics/) is a solid CB Insights report on the psychographics. Broadly, it’s our personal data preferences, which is exceptionally valuable. Just ask Google and Facebook. You can actually access some of your IG intel by going to Settings — Security — Access Data — Ads Interests. If you own this info globally, you’d be able to craft stories and market messages to win over any audience. You’d be able to build culture.) We’re conditioned creatures, which is why I think [implicit-association tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implicit-association_test) (IATs) are going to be more common practice for categorization purposes in due time. More to come, this stuff is beyond me. I’ll leave with this: To exist, is it to think, or to be perceived? That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week.
