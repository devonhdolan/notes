# Issue 104

Hello\! Last week of this road trip and I’m writing from the beautiful north coast of Michigan. Reflecting on my summer in LA, and I’d say that backend buyouts by streamers were near top of the list of discussion, paired with agency owned or affiliated financiers / studios and the writer’s strike. I wrote about this in issue 87\. I can’t escape thinking about it after seeing all these massive library licenses (ha, remember the magic of getting to 100 episodes and the promise of syndication), Netflix [pondering](https://www.indiewire.com/2019/09/netflix-bonuses-box-office-viewers-awards-1202175318/)\* bonuses based on performance kinda like Amazon’s retired Festival Stars program (the data Netflix won’t share, though Shonda and Murphy allegedly see backend), and [this](https://www.latimes.com/entertainment-arts/business/story/2019-09-12/disney-tv-shows-backend-profit-participation-changes) recent LAT article about Disney limiting profit participation. Why play for the upside if there is none? Well, there is a “new” workaround if you have enough clout. Reps are mimicking the Pollock Play that worked out alright for George Lucas — tie up ownership in derivative and character rights (merch, toys, spinoffs, etc). [Here](https://deadline.com/2015/12/star-wars-franchise-george-lucas-historic-rights-deal-tom-pollock-1201669419/) is an interview with the lawyer himself. It is one of the most famous and inventive deals of all time. So say you’re a AAA list director and a streamer brings you on as a commissioned creative to produce a series, and if you create new characters (that is what these universe are comprised of anyways) for the show, then you’ll be affiliated with them forever. Pretty neat. More on this stuff as I hear about it. And thanks Shane for sliding over [this](https://gimletmedia.com/shows/reply-all/v4he6k) great Reply All investigative podcast that touched on aspects of recent emails, from sim swapping, two factor authentication, and social media and website domain buying. That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. See you next week, from NYC.
