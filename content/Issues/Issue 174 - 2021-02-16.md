# Issue 174

Hello\! Alright, so I might get in a bit of hot water for this opener, but I gotta say it \- so many of my industry friends are helpless. If we’ve spoken recently, no this isn’t about you. These are general observations. We’re all in this godawful business to bring some meaningful stories to life (I hope) through film, tv, theatre, podcasts, whatever. But it seems that whenever I talk to anyone who’s ‘in it’ they are trying to change jobs. They whine, unhappy, believing they deserve better. The “have you heard of anything” line is now just implied in any catch-up call. It is a joke.  
Some of these cats are salaried, full-time VPs on up, to ostensibly “produce” and they literally work for the most famous and/or richest people in the world. The power vacuums. Reminder: just because you might represent or be employed by a fancy name, does not make you one… re-read that if you need to. But, put simply, the job is to make something out of nothing. From words on a piece of paper to a cinematic event.  
And you’re likely being subsidized by a first look deal, your firm’s back catalogue, a high-net worth (who prob hasn’t seen a return/distributions), or maybe even your company’s advisory division (how I stay afloat)… or dare I say it, the reality tv department. \*Gasps\* It’s near impossible to reap what you sow when commissions and fees for film and tv are needles lost in the thicket, especially in the age of the plague.  
Yet, we’re on the hunt. To identify and breath life into story. Ideally projects that are emblematic of our tastes, identity, purpose, being. And most of us don’t have a single credit to our name (looks in mirror, cries) and many have been ‘in the business’ for nearly a decade. Keep at it. Motor along. Self-actualize through the process, not the name of your boss or company. The right people will take note. The industry knows who did the impossible work of the capital P producer. Nose to grindstone. The tides will turn, eventually.  
But many don’t do the work. People are lazy. I recently met a nice furloughed dev exec who was down and out. I asked them what they’re all about. What they’ve done. Their main claim to fame was that they knew a senior talent agent and read the Tag article ([WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/articles/SB10001424127887323375204578269991660836834)) before the bidding war ensued and film came to fruition. Congrats. No hip-pocketed projects to their name. No nothing. I couldn’t help. A month or so later, they’re now highly paid earning six figs running development for a major company. And they still have no credits to their name. Cool job though.  
Any who, I believe a lot of this ill sentiment and desire to play for a different team is grounded in management… or the lack thereof. No leadership. Give your staff some autonomy. Reward them for doing the job. Compensate fairly. Don’t allow a few breadwinners to sponsor the dead weight. This reminds me of the one and [only secret guest post from issue 139](https://devonhdolan.substack.com/p/issue-139?). A good one. And if you’re trying to ‘break-in’ without any experience to your name. Good luck. Runaway. My advice? Work at Google for a decade then retire. Write a \$10k ticket into development for a project and see where it goes. Poof, gone. Ouch.  
To draft off this, I get a fair amount of inquiries about film finance, many want an intro to my old employer just for the sake of prospective employment (if you do this, I will trash your email). Here’s a hint. I’d surmise that a big reason I got my job there was because I came with a bird in hand. A self-generated initiative. On my own time in the summer of 2013, I prepared a (probably mediocre) report on the problems that filmmakers and producers faced when raising on Indiegogo and Kickstarter.  
I had direct experience. In 2012, I was a producer’s assistant \- I mean, unpaid intern \- on The Canyons. Oh the stories from that set. It was one of the first features to fully embrace crowdfunding. Go indie. Here’s the [Kickstarter](https://www.kickstarter.com/projects/1094772583/the-canyons). It was helmed by some of my fave filmmakers and writers: Paul Schrader (Taxi Driver, Raging Bull, First Reformed) and Bret Easton Ellis (Less than Zero, American Psycho). It was a dream job. I cold-emailed saying I’d jump on a grenade to work for them for free. I took on more debt. I got it. I learned a thing or two.  
Fast forward to summer 2013\. For fun, I interviewed about a hundred people asking their pain points with their campaigns. Number one: Audience Reach. They were starving for potential backers. They needed marketing help... it is the powerhouse of the cell. “If you make it, they will come” doesn’t ring true for many. I forget some of the other findings, but I do think this played to my advantage in the Cinetic interviews. I got the gig. Unbeknownst to me, Indiegogo was a client, and we later helped orchestrate the multi-million dollar raise for Super Troopers 2 [on Indiegogo](https://www.indiegogo.com/projects/super-troopers-2#/) and organized this roundtable discussion called the Crowdfunding Council ([THR](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/crowdfundings-next-bold-move-allow-819242)) with some big names. I was a big-eyed, open-eared fly on the wall. Fun stuff.  
To wrap this up. Take initiative. Be resourceful, ahead of the curve, self-sufficient. Do the work. Repeat. Do the work. Make ends meet. Cover the essentials. Keep overhead low. Bring the bread to the table. The goods.  
And finally, I’m absolutely thrilled to share that I got my NZ resident visa yesterday. Champagne was popped. My head hurts. So relieved. I must say I have a totally newfound respect for anyone that has to deal with immigration officials re visa/sponsorship issues. It is the biggest pain in the ass. I represented myself, amateur attorney style. You have to write in crayon for immigration officers and your whole life is under the thumb of some desk jockey who probably hates their job. I’d rather be audited. Hell, it borderline is a social audit but they don’t acknowledge photo essays, shared leases, and more. It is ludicrous. I don’t mind talking a bit of smack on the process now that I’m on the other side of it.  
On a very positive note, I have healthcare again\! I rolled the dice for the last year and a half like a complete nut. Now I might have to go buy a dirt bike (sorry Mom). The visa process is all-consuming \- and I’m so accustomed to hearing “no” so I was half expecting it \- now I can open up the throttle in the promised land of the sheep. There is some covid here now (3 cases), we’ve been on lockdown in Auckland the last three days, but have a feeling we’ll open up again soon. Sorry, rambling. I’m just very excited and wanted to yell into the void of this newsletter :)  
That’s all for now. Thanks for reading. Everything is a game. See you next week.
